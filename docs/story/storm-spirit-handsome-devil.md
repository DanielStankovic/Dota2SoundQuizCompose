# Who's That Handsome Devil?

A seven-part comedy about Storm Spirit, told in simple English. The genre is a **romcom that never
becomes a romance**: one terrible first date, told in real time, with a man who is completely
certain it is going well.

Each part is roughly 100 words, one part per Hero Journey level (parts 1–7 → levels 1–7).

Companion stories: `lina-golden-rolling-pin.md`, `juggernaut-sixty-one-carvings.md`,
`pudge-big-papa-meat.md`, `sniper-eleven-bullets.md`.

---

## Localisation notes

**Hero names are NEVER translated.** Keep them exactly as written, in every language:

- Storm Spirit
- Raijin Thunderkeg
- Muerta

Items that *should* be translated, with care:

- **"The name is Spirit. Storm Spirit."** — Only "The name is" gets translated. **"Spirit" and
  "Storm Spirit" stay exactly as written**, because they are the hero name, and hero names are
  never translated. Keep the same two-step structure: surname alone, then full name.
  **The line must be word-for-word identical in Part 2 and Part 7**, because the repetition is the
  ending of the whole story. If it differs between the two, the joke is gone.
- **"Ooh, who's that handsome devil?"** (Part 1) — the story title comes from this line. Translate
  the line and the title the same way, so they match.
- **"You look a bit... dead. Like a fancy corpse!"** (Part 3) — keep the pause before "dead". The
  joke is that he thinks this is a charming observation.
- **"Hey, slowpoke!"** and **"I am a thirsty storm!"** (Part 4) — rude and childish, not menacing.
  Use whatever a loud, self-satisfied customer would shout at a waiter in that language.
- **"Her loss."** (Part 6) — must be short, dismissive and said under the breath. Two words if the
  language allows it.
- All part titles.

There is **no swearing** in this story. Do not add any.

Tone guidance for translators: keep sentences short and words everyday. **This story is an
exception to the no-dialogue rule used in the other stories** — the dialogue is the comedy, because
Storm Spirit says every single thing out loud. Do not convert it to narration.

The humour is cringe. Everything he says is insulting, and he never once notices. Keep his lines
cheerful and confident, never sarcastic or mean — he genuinely believes he is being charming, and
the joke stops working the moment he sounds like he knows better. Muerta says very little; keep her
lines short, cold and controlled.

## Part 1 — The Mirror

Storm Spirit stood in front of the large mirror, fixing his bright blue armor. He was Raijin Thunderkeg, a powerful storm elemental trapped in a jolly man's body. But today, he was just a man getting ready for a date. He struck a pose, flexing his arms. He smiled widely at his reflection. "Ooh, who's that handsome devil?" he asked himself, laughing. He loved his own confidence. He put on his favorite hat and grabbed a fresh flower. He was ready to bless some lucky lady with his perfect presence. There was no way this date could go wrong.

---

## Part 2 — The Name Is Spirit

He arrived at the fancy restaurant like a burst of lightning. At the corner table, a woman was waiting. She wore dark clothes and had a very pale, ghostly face. Storm Spirit walked over with a big, cheesy grin. He leaned against the table, trying to look cool. "The name is Spirit," he said, lowering his voice. "Storm Spirit." He gave her his best mysterious smile. The woman blinked, surprised by his sudden energy. "I am Muerta," she said softly. At first, she found his extreme confidence a little charming. She pointed to the empty chair.

---

## Part 3 — A Fancy Corpse

Storm Spirit sat down and looked Muerta up and down. "Well, Muerta, you look interesting," he said. He leaned in closer. "Are you feeling okay? You look a bit... dead. Like a fancy corpse!" He laughed loudly at his own joke. Muerta did not laugh. Her eyes narrowed slightly. "I am connected to the dead," she replied coldly. Storm Spirit waved his hand. "Don't worry, my bright personality will bring you back to life!" He did not notice her annoyed sigh. He was too busy looking at his own perfect reflection in a silver spoon on the table.

---

## Part 4 — The Lady in Red

The date went from bad to worse. When the waiter brought their drinks, Storm Spirit snapped his fingers loudly. "Hey, slowpoke! Bring me a bigger glass. I am a thirsty storm!" he ordered rudely. Muerta frowned, feeling embarrassed. Then, a pretty lady in a red dress walked past their table. Storm Spirit stopped talking mid-sentence and watched the lady walk away. "Looking good!" he shouted after her, winking. He turned back to Muerta, smiling. "Did you see her dress? You should wear bright colors like that. It would help your pale skin." Muerta's hands curled into tight fists.

---

## Part 5 — Don't Feel Bad About Yourself

Storm Spirit finally noticed that Muerta was not talking. She was just staring at him with cold, glowing eyes. "What is wrong?" he asked, acting innocent. "You are very quiet." He decided it was time to lighten the mood. "Listen, I know I am a lot to handle. It is hard sitting next to perfection. But don't feel bad about yourself!" He gave her a playful punch on the arm. "We can't all be the handsome Storm Spirit!" Instead of smiling, Muerta looked furious. The air around her grew completely cold. His attempt to fix things failed completely.

---

## Part 6 — Her Loss

Muerta stood up. Her chair scraped loudly against the floor. "You are the most self-centered, obnoxious man I have ever met," she said. Her voice was calm but full of pure anger. "You only care about yourself." Without another word, she turned around and walked out of the restaurant. Storm Spirit sat frozen in his chair. He was completely confused. He blinked his eyes and looked around. Why did she leave? He had been charming, funny, and he looked amazing. He picked up his glass of water and took a sip, shaking his head. "Her loss," he muttered.

---

## Part 7 — Hello There

He sat alone for only a minute. Being sad was simply not his style. He brushed some imaginary dust off his shoulder and sat up straight. He noticed a beautiful woman sitting alone at the next table. She was quietly reading a menu. Storm Spirit slid his chair back and smoothly walked over to her table. He leaned in, resting his arm on the back of her chair. He put on that exact same cheesy grin. "Hello there," he said smoothly. "The name is Spirit. Storm Spirit."

## Story structure notes

Kept here so future edits do not accidentally break the chain.

- **One scene, told in order:** a single first date, from getting dressed to the walk-out. Nothing
  to hold in your head, which is why it works at seven short parts.
- **Storm Spirit's arc in one sentence:** there isn't one, and that is the joke. He ends the story
  exactly where he started it, doing exactly the same thing to a different woman.
- **The escalation is the structure.** Each part is one worse thing: a backhanded compliment
  (Part 3), rudeness to staff plus openly admiring another woman (Part 4), telling her not to feel
  bad about not being him (Part 5). It has to get worse every part, or the walk-out in Part 6 does
  not land.
- **He must never become self-aware.** Every line is cheerful and completely sincere. The moment he
  sounds like he knows he is being rude, the comedy dies.
- **Cliffhangers that pull the reader to the next level:** her narrowed eyes (3), her clenched
  fists (4), the air going cold (5), her standing up (5→6), and him spotting the next woman (7).
- **Setups and payoffs — do not remove these:**
  - **"The name is Spirit. Storm Spirit."** (Part 2) → repeated word-for-word as the final line of
    the story (Part 7). **This is the whole point of the story: he learned nothing, and the loop
    starts again.** The two lines must match exactly in every translation.
  - He admires his reflection in the mirror (Part 1) → he admires it again in a silver spoon while
    she is talking (Part 3).
  - Muerta says she is connected to the dead (Part 3) → the air around her goes cold (Part 5),
    which is the only warning he gets and the only one he ignores.
  - "There was no way this date could go wrong" (Part 1) → "Her loss" (Part 6).
