A twenty-part workplace comedy about Pudge, in the style of *The Office*. Pudge is the branch
manager: loud, stupid, obnoxious, desperate to be loved, and completely certain he is the coolest
man in the building. The humour is awkward and cringe — secondhand embarrassment reported in a
flat, factual voice.

Each part is roughly 100 words, one part per Hero Journey level (parts 1–20 → levels 1–20).

Companion stories: `lina-golden-rolling-pin.md`, `juggernaut-sixty-one-carvings.md`.

## Localisation notes

**Hero names are NEVER translated.** Keep them exactly as written, in every language:

- Pudge
- Tresdin / Legion Commander
- Rubick
- Dazzle
- Krobelus / Death Prophet

Other do-not-translate names:

- **Carnage Foods** — the company. Leave as-is or transliterate.
- **Marrow** — the town. Leave as-is or transliterate. Do not translate the meaning.

Items that *should* be translated, with care:

- **"Big Papa Meat"** — the nickname Pudge gave himself. This is the single most important joke in
  the story and the title. It must sound like something a middle-aged man invented for himself and
  then forced on his staff: a bit sleazy, a bit fatherly, deeply embarrassing, and clearly
  self-awarded. Do not translate it literally if the literal version is not cringeworthy. Write the
  equivalent dad-nickname in the target language. Keep it consistent in all four places it appears
  (Parts 2, 15, 20 and the title).
- **"Assistant to the Regional Manager"** — Tresdin's invented job title in Part 5. The joke is that
  it sounds important but means nothing. Keep that effect.
- **"WORLD'S GREATEST BUTCHER"** (mug, Part 1), **"FAN MAIL"** (the complaints binder, Parts 4 and
  20), **"MEAT FEST"** (the party banner, Part 9), **"WE ARE ALL MEAT INSIDE"** (the slideshow
  title, Part 10), **"ODE TO A STRONG WOMAN FROM A STRONG MAN"** (the poem, Part 11) — translate
  all of these, keeping them in capitals. They are meant to look like things Pudge typed himself.
- All part titles.

**Swearing — intentional, keep it mild.** There are four printed swears, in Parts 10, 12, 14 and 15.
They are there to puncture the awkward silence, not for shock. Translate them with the everyday
casual equivalent in the target language — the register of "oh crap" to "oh hell", not aggressive
profanity. Do not add more, do not escalate them, and do not remove them either, since each one
lands a joke.

Tone guidance for translators: keep sentences short and words everyday. The comedy is **deadpan** —
ridiculous behaviour reported flatly, with the worst detail saved for the last line of each part.
Never signal the joke. No exclamation marks in the narration, no "hilariously", no winking at the
reader. If a gag does not work in the target language, replace it with one that does; a joke that
lands matters more than a literal translation.

## Part 1 — The Mug

Pudge runs Branch 14 of Carnage Foods, in Marrow. Thirty-one staff, one car park, one vending machine that only does crisps.

On his desk is a mug that says **WORLD'S GREATEST BUTCHER**. He tells people it was a gift from the team. He bought it himself at the airport. The receipt is still in his top drawer, and he has handed the mug to so many people that most of the branch has now seen the receipt.

He believes he is the funniest man in the building.

He is correct that he is the loudest.

---

## Part 2 — Big Papa Meat

In 2019 he decided his nickname would be Big Papa Meat.

Nobody calls him that. He has given it four years. He signs his emails that way. He ordered a second nameplate with it on, at company expense, and when Finance queried the invoice he described it as branding.

He refers to himself in the third person in meetings, in the hope it catches on.

Once he answered his desk phone as Big Papa Meat and it was the tax office. He stayed in character for about forty seconds, then asked if he could start the call again.

They said no.

---

## Part 3 — The Morning Briefing

Every morning at 8:55 he does twenty minutes on the public address system.

It covers the weather, his own dreams, and whatever he had for dinner. He does voices. He has a recurring character that he does in a high accent, and nobody in the building knows who it is supposed to be, including, several people suspect, him.

One Thursday he described a dream involving the entire sales team, in detail, for nine minutes.

Three people have since requested a move to the second floor, where the speaker has been broken since spring.

He believes they are asking for more responsibility.

---

## Part 4 — The Fun Police

Dazzle is People and Wellness. Pudge calls him the fun police, to his face, every single time he sees him, and laughs alone each time.

In March he moved Dazzle's desk into the walk-in freezer. He announced it as a promotion, because it is technically a corner, and therefore, in Pudge's words, a corner office.

Dazzle now works in a parka. He has filed sixty-one formal complaints.

Pudge keeps all sixty-one in a ring binder on the shelf behind his chair, where visitors can see it. He has labelled the spine **FAN MAIL**.

He points at it during job interviews.

---

## Part 5 — Assistant to the Regional Manager

Tresdin's job title is Assistant to the Regional Manager. That position does not exist at Carnage Foods. She made the badge herself and wears it on a lanyard.

She keeps a sword under her desk and two spare swords in the stationery cupboard, behind the envelopes.

In August she challenged a supplier to single combat over a late delivery of pork. Pudge approved it. In writing. On letterhead.

In her last performance review, under *goals for the coming year*, she wrote that she would die for him.

He read it out at the Christmas party. He thought it was about the company.

---

## Part 6 — Rubick

Rubick does sales. Rubick appears to do nothing.

He replaced Tresdin's sword with a foam one from a toy shop. She fought the supplier with it and won, which Pudge has described as a victory for the branch.

He keeps a small card in his wallet with a tally on it. The tally is things Pudge has said. He has never explained the card to anyone and nobody has asked.

What also nobody knows is that for the past year, at seven o'clock most evenings, after everyone has gone, Rubick quietly reopens the branch spreadsheets and fixes them.

---

## Part 7 — The Quarterly Review

Krobelus came up from corporate in October to review the branch.

She is a Death Prophet. She wears grey. She told the room, without blinking, that everything in it would one day be dust, including the room.

Pudge thought they had a connection.

He thought the review went great. At one point he winked at her for so long that Dazzle looked away.

At the end she said, flatly, that he would die in this building.

He had it printed, framed, and hung in reception under the word **COMMITMENT**.

He tells visitors it is about him and her.

---

## Part 8 — Trust Falls

For team building he took all thirty-one of them onto the cutting floor and made them do trust falls.

Tresdin caught everybody. Nobody asked her to. She caught two people who were not falling.

Pudge went last, backwards, onto Dazzle.

There were three injuries, one of them a tooth. The branch was down a day and a half of production and somebody's cardigan was never found.

He describes it as the best day this branch has ever had, and he brings it up at least monthly.

On the incident report, in the box marked *cause*, he wrote: **fun**.

---

## Part 9 — Meat Fest

In November he spent the branch's entire annual facilities budget on a party.

It was called **MEET FEST**, because the banner came back misspelled and he decided it was a pun. There was an ice sculpture of his own head. The DJ was his cousin and played four songs on a loop.

Because the facilities budget was gone, the first-floor toilets could not be fixed until April.

He still calls it the greatest night of their lives.

Two members of staff have since left the meat industry entirely, and one of them mentions the party by name on her blog.

---

## Part 10 — We Are All Meat Inside

Dazzle booked a sensitivity training session. Pudge took over after four minutes, on the grounds that he *knows people*.

He did impressions. Several impressions. He did one for nine seconds, and then stopped, because a woman in the second row had put her head in her hands.

Then he delivered a slideshow he had made at home, titled **WE ARE ALL MEAT INSIDE**, which included his own X-ray and a photograph of a cow.

From somewhere near the back, very clearly, someone said: *oh, fuck.*

He said thank you.

Dazzle filed complaint sixty-two from inside the room, on his phone.

---

## Part 11 — Ode to a Strong Woman

Krobelus stayed at the Marrow Arms for two nights. He found this out from her assistant, by pretending to be a courier.

At one in the morning he slid eleven verses of original poetry under her hotel door. It was titled **ODE TO A STRONG WOMAN FROM A STRONG MAN**. He was wearing a great deal of cologne over the top of himself, which, combined, has been described by two witnesses as structural.

He also left meat.

She has never once mentioned any of it.

He has taken this to mean that they are seeing each other, and has told four people.

---

## Part 12 — The Email

In January corporate sent a spreadsheet to the wrong list. Marrow was on a tab called *Closures — Phase One*.

Pudge read it at 9:12am and decided to protect his family by keeping it from them.

At 9:51am he called an all-hands and told them. By singing it. He had rewritten a well-known song, with Marrow in it instead of the original words, and he performed all of it standing on a chair.

Halfway through he got choked up at his own lyrics and had to stop.

Then he said *but we're fine*, and left the room.

Somebody at the back said: *oh fuck*.

---

## Part 13 — The Plan

His plan to save the branch was to break a world record.

Specifically, to produce the longest continuous sausage ever made. He told the local newspaper on the Monday. He told the staff on the Wednesday.

He asked all thirty-one of them to give up a weekend, unpaid, *as a family*. He provided refreshments, which turned out to be one cake, with his own face printed on it in icing, which he then cut up himself and handed out while maintaining eye contact.

Twenty-nine of them came in.

Tresdin came in on Friday night and slept in the building, to be first.

---

## Part 14 — One Thousand Four Hundred and Twelve Metres

They did it. One thousand four hundred and twelve metres, looped three times around the car park, in the rain.

A reporter came with a photographer. He photographed Pudge kissing it.

On camera, the reporter asked what the record meant to him personally, and Pudge gave an answer that was so inappropriate, in so many directions at once, that the clip has now been watched four million times and is still going.

The photographer can be heard saying *daaamn* under his breath.

Enquiries to Branch 14 went up three hundred percent that month.

Nobody at Carnage Foods has ever been able to explain it.

---

## Part 15 — Jesus Christ, Pudge

Corporate sent an efficiency consultant in February. Grey suit, grey folder, two days.

Pudge met him in the car park, gave him a nickname within eleven seconds, and hugged him for nineteen.

Then he introduced himself, formally, hand extended, as Big Papa Meat.

The consultant wrote it down.

Tresdin offered, twice, to resolve the whole audit by single combat, and had to be walked back to her desk.

From behind a monitor, not quietly enough, Rubick said: *Jesus fucking Christ, Pudge.*

Pudge gave him a thumbs up, because he thought he was being encouraged, and then did it again at lunch.

---

## Part 16 — Seven O'Clock

The consultant could not find anything wrong with the numbers.

He looked for two days. The forecasts were clean, the stock reconciliation was clean, the margins were correct to the penny. For a branch run by a man who files his receipts inside the mug, it was immaculate.

It was immaculate because for one year, at seven o'clock most evenings, Rubick has been sitting back down and redoing all of it.

The consultant asked him why he does that.

Rubick said he genuinely did not know.

It is the most honest thing he has said at work in eleven years.

---

## Part 17 — Eleven Years

Then the other number came out of the audit, and it stopped the room.

Branch 14 has not lost a single client in eleven years. Not one. Nowhere else in the company comes close.

Because Pudge does the deliveries himself. He knows the names of their children. He knows who had the knee operation and who is getting divorced and whose dog died in the spring, and he turns up to the funerals, in a suit, and he stands at the back.

Some of them are a little frightened of him.

Most of them are not, and that is the part the company cannot copy.

---

## Part 18 — The Presentation

They flew him to head office to argue for the branch. He prepared forty slides, thirty-eight of which were photographs of himself.

Then he unplugged the projector to charge a sandwich press, and that was the end of the slides.

So he stood in front of the board with nothing, for six minutes, and talked about thirty-one people in Marrow. By name. One at a time. What each of them is good at, and what each of them has been through this year.

He did not make a single joke.

When he finished nobody spoke, and then a man from accounts cried.

---

## Part 19 — Dinner

They kept Branch 14 open.

Krobelus signed the decision herself and wrote one line in the margin, which somebody photographed and which is now a shared document: *Keep it. He amuses me.*

Then she agreed to have dinner with him.

He screamed. In the open-plan office. For about nine seconds, with his fists clenched down by his sides, while thirty-one people watched.

He has since told everybody it is a business dinner, with a wink so prolonged that two people asked, seriously, whether he needed a doctor.

He bought a new shirt. He has shown everyone the receipt for that too.

---

## Part 20 — Fan Mail

Branch 14 is still open.

The sausage is in the lobby in a glass case. It has gone grey. He points at it during job interviews, next to the binder.

Dazzle is still in the freezer, with a space heater now, and ninety-four complaints in the folder marked **FAN MAIL**. Tresdin still has a job title that does not exist. Rubick still sits back down at seven o'clock, and Pudge still has no idea.

And once, in December, one person said *morning, Big Papa Meat*, as a joke, in passing.

He had to go and sit in his car for a while.

## Story structure notes

Kept here so future edits do not accidentally break the chain.

- **The character:** Pudge is Michael Scott. He is stupid, loud and obnoxious, he thinks he is
  hilarious and beloved, and he is wrong about almost everything — except that he does genuinely
  love these people, which is why the ending works. Never let him be self-aware. He must never
  realise a joke has failed. He always reads silence as approval.
- **How each part is built:** one concrete awkward act, reported flatly, with the most
  embarrassing detail held back for the final line. The comedy is in the detail, not the event —
  it is not funny that he bought his own mug, it is funny that people have seen the receipt.
- **The structure is a sitcom season.** Parts 1–6 are character introductions, each one a
  self-contained gag so early levels land on their own. Parts 7–11 escalate. Part 12 starts the
  real plot (the branch may close). Parts 13–15 are his terrible plan. Parts 16–19 turn it, and the
  turn is sincere. Part 20 goes back to jokes, then lands the heart on the last line.
- **Cringe peaks:** the dream monologue (3), the frozen desk and FAN MAIL binder (4), the framed
  death threat (7), the X-ray slideshow (10), the hotel poem (11), singing the closure news (12),
  the face cake (13), the interview clip (14), the nineteen-second hug (15), the scream (19).
- **Setups and payoffs — do not remove these:**
  - The mug and the receipt (Part 1) → he shows everyone the receipt for his new shirt (Part 19).
  - "Big Papa Meat" (Part 2) → he introduces himself to the consultant that way (Part 15) → one
    colleague finally says it as a joke and it destroys him (Part 20). **This is the ending of the
    whole story. Everything else is in service of that last line.**
  - The FAN MAIL binder, 61 complaints (Part 4) → 62 filed from inside the room (Part 10) → 94 and
    still growing (Part 20).
  - Tresdin's fake job title and her "I would die for him" review (Part 5) → she sleeps in the
    building to be first in (Part 13) → she offers to settle the audit by combat (Part 15).
  - Rubick's seven o'clock spreadsheet habit, planted quietly (Part 6) → it is why the audit finds
    nothing (Part 16) → he still does it and Pudge still does not know (Part 20).
  - Krobelus says he will die in this building (Part 7) → he frames it as COMMITMENT → she keeps
    the branch open because he amuses her and agrees to dinner (Part 19).
  - The world-record sausage (Parts 13–14) → it is in the lobby in a glass case, grey (Part 20).
- **The two sincere parts are Part 17 and Part 18, and they must stay sincere.** No jokes in the
  six-minute speech. The whole comedy only pays off if the reader suddenly realises that the idiot
  is the best manager in the company, and that it is for a reason no spreadsheet can measure.
