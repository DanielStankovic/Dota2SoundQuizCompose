# The Satyr's Strange Dota Match

A twenty-two-part story about Riki, told in simple English. It is a **pub isekai**: a satyr
takes one gulp of cheap beer, the room vanishes, and he wakes up in a match he does not
understand, with a stick for a sword and a paper that says destroy the Ancient. Comedy first,
then a real fight, then the punchline on the pub table.

Each part is roughly 75 words and is meant to be delivered one part per Hero Journey level
(parts 1–22 → levels 1–22). **These parts are shorter than the 100-word stories in this
folder, and this is the first 22-part story** — that is how it was written and it is
deliberate. If parts are ever expanded, expand all twenty-two, not some. Do not cut it down
to 15 or 20 to match the others.

Companion stories: `lina-golden-rolling-pin.md`, `juggernaut-sixty-one-carvings.md`,
`pudge-butcher-of-blackwater.md`, `sniper-eleven-bullets.md`,
`tinker-the-cupboard-kids.md`, `invoker-three-wisps.md`, `rubick-the-wooden-puppet.md`.

Note for future agents: the fire-and-ice twin sisters in Parts 9–17 are Lina and Rylai. Lina
has her own story in `lina-golden-rolling-pin.md`, where they are bakers, not lane opponents.
Different world, different people. Do not merge them, do not import Icewrack Valley, and do
not name the sisters Lina or Rylai in this file — the player is meant to recognise them from
fire, ice, and the fact that they are twins.

Note on sources: Liquipedia blocks the fetch tool with a human-verification page, so the hero
details here come from known Dota 2 canon (Riki's smoke and backstabs, Nature's Prophet's
tree rings, Faceless Void's time bubble, the fountain, creeps, the courier, Yasha, First
Blood, Rampage, the Radiant Ancient and the Dire Ancient), not from a live lore read.

## Localisation notes

**Hero names are NEVER translated.** Keep them exactly as written, in every language:

- Riki
- Faceless Void
- Void (short for Faceless Void, Parts 18 only)
- Furion
- Nature's Prophet

Notes on these:

- **Riki** is never translated and never replaced with a local name.
- **Faceless Void** is the official hero name. **Use the official in-game localised version.**
  Part 4 is where he introduces himself, so that line is how the reader locks the name.
- **Void** in Part 18 is the same person, shortened the way a teammate would. Use the
  locale's natural short form of the official Faceless Void name, or repeat the full name if
  a short form would be unclear. Do not invent a nickname.
- **Furion** is Nature's Prophet's personal name. Keep "Furion". If a locale has an official
  transliteration, use it.
- **"the Nature's Prophet"** (Part 4). This is his official title. **Use the official in-game
  localised version of Nature's Prophet**, exactly. Keep it as a title he claims, not as a
  job description.

The story never names these four, on purpose. Do not add the names in translation:

- the old man with the brown bear (Lone Druid)
- the wild man with two wolves (Lycan)
- the fire sister (Lina)
- the ice sister (Rylai / Crystal Maiden)

Players are meant to work them out. Adding the names ruins that.

Other do-not-translate names:

- **Yasha** (Parts 12, 15, 19, 22, and the Part 12 title). This is a real Dota item. **Use the
  official in-game localised item name**, exactly, in every language. It is carved into a
  sword handle and then seen through torn paper — both times it has to be the same word a
  player reads in their item tooltip. Never translate it as "speed" or "agility".

Items that *should* be translated, with care:

- **"Ancient"** (Parts 6, 8, 16, 20, and the Part 16 title). The enemy building. **Use the
  official in-game localised word for the Ancient.** Same word every time. It is a building,
  not a person and not "an old thing".
- **"Radiant"** (Part 17). Riki's team. **Use the official in-game localised faction name.**
- **"First Blood"** (Part 10, and the Part 10 title). The announcer phrase. **Use the official
  in-game localised announcer line**, exactly. Furion shouts it as a warning, not as a
  tutorial. Keep the capitals if the game does.
- **"Rampage"** (Part 20, and the Part 20 title). The announcer phrase for five kills. **Use
  the official in-game localised announcer line**, exactly. In the story it is a voice from
  the sky. Do not translate it as "a killing spree" or "five kills". The point is that Riki
  hears a game word and thinks it is thunder.
- **"creeps"** (Parts 7, 8, 11, and the Part 7 title). Riki and Furion invent this name on
  the page, but it is the real in-game word. **Use the official in-game localised word for
  lane creeps.** Keep the quotation marks in Part 7, where they name them. After that, no
  quotes. Do not use a word that only means "unpleasant person".
- **"courier"** (Part 12) and **"town courier"** (Part 22). Part 12 is the flying shop animal.
  Part 22 is a man who delivers packages in a town. The whole ending depends on the reader
  feeling they are the same job. Use one everyday word for a delivery runner in both parts
  if the official in-game courier name would make Part 22 sound like a flying donkey walked
  into a pub. If you use the official courier name in Part 12, Part 22 still needs to read
  as a normal man at a pub door. The joke is the job, not the animal.
- **"wooden branch" / "stick"** (Parts 6, 8). This is the item Iron Branch, hidden as a
  useless stick. Translate as a plain stick from a tree. Do not insert the item name.
- **"smoke bomb" / "smoke"** (Parts 14, 15). This is Riki's Smoke Screen. Translate as a
  prank smoke bomb a person could buy, not as a spell name. Keep it purple in Part 15.
  Purple is Riki's colour. Do not change it.
- **"purple bubble" / "time bubble"** (Parts 18, 19, and the Part 18 title). This is
  Faceless Void's Chronosphere. Translate as a bubble that freezes time. Do not insert the
  ability name.
- **"circles of trees" / "ring of tall trees"** (Parts 13, 14, 15). This is Nature's
  Prophet's Sprout. Translate as trees growing in a circle that trap people. Do not insert
  the ability name.
- **"fountain"** (Parts 2, 5). The glowing base fountain. Translate as a fountain. Keep
  "glowing".
- **"gold" / "gold coins"** (Parts 3, 6, 11). Game gold. Use the locale's normal word for
  gold coins. Do not convert it into a real currency.
- **"south front"** (Parts 6, 7). The bottom lane. Translate as the south side of the
  fight, not as "bot lane" or other player slang.
- All part titles. **"First Blood"**, **"Yasha"** and **"Rampage"** follow the rules above,
  including in titles.
- **"The Satyr's Strange Dota Match"** — the story title. Translate *satyr*, *strange* and
  *match*. **Keep "Dota" exactly as written** in every language; it is the game's name, not
  a word. Do not replace it with "DotA", "Defense of the Ancients", or a description of the
  game. The title may say Dota. The story body still must not.

### The cheeky lines and the idioms

- **"smoking hot body with great cleavage"** and **"awesome booty"** and **"looked even
  hotter"** (Part 9). These are deliberate. Keep them cheeky, the way a drunk satyr would
  talk, not pornographic and not polite. Use the everyday words people in that language
  actually use for a body they are staring at. Do not delete them, do not soften them into
  "very pretty", and do not make them more explicit. The ice sister being "even hotter"
  is a small joke because she is made of ice — keep that contrast if the language lets
  you. If "hotter" only means temperature, write a new short joke with the same idea:
  the cold one is the one he cannot stop looking at.
- **"Looking to buy, little friend?"** (Part 3). A shady salesman. Keep it greedy, not
  friendly.
- **"Just whistle and get your courier to bring it. Why walk?"** (Part 12) and **"Why are
  you holding all that money? Buy a weapon!"** (Part 11). Furion is impatient, not kind.
  Keep him nagging.
- **"What the hell are you doing?"** (Part 10). See swearing below.
- **"You look like you've seen a ghost"** (Part 21). A common idiom. **Replace it with the
  equivalent idiom in the target language.** Do not translate the words.
- **"ticket back to his local pub"** (Part 16). Means "the only way home". Use the locale's
  normal phrase for that.

**Swearing — there is exactly one line, and it must stay.** Part 10: *hell*, in Furion's
shout. Use the everyday casual equivalent, not aggressive profanity. Do not add more, do
not remove it. This story is cheeky and bloody, but it is not the adult Queen of Pain
file; do not raise the heat.

### Style guidance — simple English is a hard rule

Most readers are **not native English speakers**, and the text will be machine-translated
into many languages. The English in this file is deliberately plain. Keep it that way when
editing, and match it when translating.

- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence.
- **Everyday words only.** The file says *weird*, not bizarre. *Stick*, not staff. If a
  simpler word exists, it is the right word.
- **No literary or poetic language.**
- **Do not raise the register when translating.** If your translation sounds like a school
  textbook, it is wrong.
- **This story uses dialogue, and that is allowed.** Furion nags, the merchant sells, a
  friend in the pub asks if Riki is okay, and the sky says *Rampage*. Keep those lines.
  Do not convert them to narration and do not add more.
- **Purple stays purple** (the smoke, the bubble, Void himself). **The enemy Ancient stays
  fiery.** **The fountain stays glowing.** Those colours are how a player maps the scene
  onto a real match.

## Part 1 — Cheap Beer

Riki was just a regular satyr, enjoying a quiet night with his friends. They sat in their favorite local pub, drinking cheap beer and sharing simple stories. Riki took a long gulp from his wooden mug, feeling completely relaxed. Then, the room suddenly vanished. A blinding flash of pure white light swallowed the pub, the tables, and his friends. Before he could even scream, the satyr felt his feet lift off the ground completely.

---

## Part 2 — The Fountain

When Riki opened his eyes, he was nowhere near the pub. He stood on stone blocks in a lush, magical clearing. Behind him stood a giant, glowing fountain. It poured crystal-clear water and emitted a warm, positive energy that instantly cured his headache. Riki rubbed his eyes, totally confused. Everything felt too real to be a dream, but far too weird to be reality. He wondered if his friends had spiked his dark beer.

---

## Part 3 — Looking to Buy

To his right, Riki noticed a small wooden stall. A shady, hooded merchant leaned over the counter, smiling greedily. The man opened his cloak to reveal shiny swords, magical potions, and heavy armor. "Looking to buy, little friend?" the merchant whispered. Riki checked his pockets and found a small pouch of unfamiliar gold coins. He stared at the fancy, glowing weapons, quickly realizing his tiny handful of gold was not nearly enough for them.

---

## Part 4 — Not Satyrs

Riki stepped back and noticed four other people standing nearby. They were definitely not satyrs. One was a muscular purple creature holding a giant, heavy mace. Strangely, the creature had no eyes or mouth at all. He introduced himself as Faceless Void. Beside him stood a very tall man holding a twisted wooden staff. The man called himself Furion, the Nature's Prophet, and claimed he could actually speak directly to the surrounding forest trees.

---

## Part 5 — The Paper

The other two teammates were just as strange. One was an old man walking beside a massive, terrifying brown bear. The last guy looked wild and had two fierce wolves pacing at his feet. Riki felt his head spin. Was there something in that pub beer? Suddenly, a small piece of paper floated down into his hands. It was a set of instructions. He was chosen to defend the giant, glowing tree behind the fountain.

---

## Part 6 — A Stick

The paper told Riki to go to the south front with Furion. To end this nightmare, they had to break through to the enemy's Ancient building and destroy it permanently. Riki looked at his small pouch of starter gold. Since he could not afford the shady guy's fancy swords, he just bought a simple wooden branch. It was practically a stick, but it would have to do as a weapon for his journey now.

---

## Part 7 — Creeps

Riki and Furion walked down to the southern front. When they arrived, small groups of strange people were already fighting. There were five on each side. Four fought closely with tiny swords, while one stood in the back throwing magical rocks. Riki watched them move. They crept around so strangely that he and Furion agreed to just call them "creeps." Furion seemed very calm and experienced, so Riki stayed close to ask him for advice.

---

## Part 8 — The Killing Blow

When a friendly creep severely wounded an enemy creep, the enemy turned and attacked Riki. Panicking, Riki swung his small wooden branch and struck a killing blow. The enemy fell dead. Riki felt sick, sad, and confused. He had never killed anyone before. He picked up the dead creep's short dagger, realizing his branch was useless. He reminded himself of the paper: he had to push through to their Ancient to finally go home.

---

## Part 9 — Twin Sisters

As Riki got used to the dagger, two taller figures stepped out of the dark woods. They were twin sisters, and both were incredibly beautiful. The first one had a smoking hot body with great cleavage, and her hands literally burned with bright red fire. The second sister had an awesome booty and, ironically, looked even hotter than the first. However, everywhere she walked, the ground froze into a solid layer of white snow.

---

## Part 10 — First Blood

Riki thought about saying hello and throwing a friendly joke. But before he could even smile, he was trapped inside a freezing block of ice. A second later, the ground beneath him erupted in scorching fire. Riki panicked, broke free, and ran away in terror. Furion yelled from the trees, "What the hell are you doing? They are the enemies! Do you want them to get First Blood on you?" Riki just kept running.

---

## Part 11 — Buy a Weapon

Riki slowly learned how to fight. He avoided the fire and ice moves the sisters kept throwing at him. He was getting exhausted, and the short dagger was terrible. However, he noticed dropped gold coins near the defeated creeps. He picked them up, remembering the beautiful swords in the shop. Furion sighed loudly. "Why are you holding all that money? Buy a weapon!" Riki explained the shop was much too far away to run back.

---

## Part 12 — Yasha

Furion looked at Riki in total disbelief. "Just whistle and get your courier to bring it. Why walk?" Riki was incredibly confused but whistled anyway. A few moments later, a small, flying donkey arrived carrying a beautifully crafted katana. Riki took the sword. It felt incredibly light but very strong in his hands. He read the shiny inscription carved into the handle. It simply said "Yasha." It felt absolutely perfect for a fast satyr.

---

## Part 13 — Fire and Ice

With his new sword, Riki tried to fight the twin sisters again. Furion used his magic to grow circles of trees, trapping the girls inside. Riki tried to sneak up and strike them from the back. But every single time, the fire sister burned the trees down, or the ice sister froze them and escaped. It was a very hard match against the joined forces of fire and ice. They desperately needed a new strategy.

---

## Part 14 — Smoke Bombs

Furion thought long and hard. He noticed Riki carried small, strange pouches on his belt. Riki explained he bought them from a traveling merchant years ago just to play pranks on his friends back home. They were heavy smoke bombs. Furion smiled. He suggested trapping the sisters in the trees again, but this time Riki would throw a smoke bomb inside. The thick smoke would disorient them and stop them from using their magic.

---

## Part 15 — One Slice

The plan worked perfectly. Furion trapped the sisters in a sudden ring of tall trees. Riki instantly threw his smoke bomb. The thick purple cloud filled the trap. The sisters started coughing violently, completely blinded and unable to cast their spells. Riki leaped into the smoke and delivered two fast, fatal blows with a single slice of Yasha. He wasn't proud of killing them, but he was very happy to be the one surviving.

---

## Part 16 — Ticket Home

With the sisters defeated, the path was clear. Riki and his companions pushed deep into enemy territory. Finally, they reached the enemy Ancient. It was a massive, terrifying structure. The fiery building crackled with dark, evil energy and cast twisted shadows on the ground. Riki knew that destroying this scary building was his only ticket back to his local pub. The group stepped forward, ready to strike the dark stone and end the battle.

---

## Part 17 — Reborn

Suddenly, five enemies stepped in front of the dark building to defend it. Riki gasped. The twin sisters of fire and ice were standing right there, fully healed. It was like they were magically reborn. Riki was completely baffled, but he had no time to think. All five enemies charged forward together. Riki quickly looked around and realized Faceless Void was nowhere to be seen. The Radiant team was heavily outnumbered and in trouble.

---

## Part 18 — The Purple Bubble

Just as the enemy team prepared to strike, Void leaped out from the dark woods. He raised his giant mace and cast a massive, glowing purple bubble over the five enemies. The moment the bubble formed, the enemies completely froze. They stopped moving, stopped attacking, and even stopped breathing. They were entirely stuck in time and space. Riki stood still, totally amazed by the unbelievable power his faceless friend had just fully displayed.

---

## Part 19 — While They Can't Move

"Come on, Riki! What are you waiting for? Slice them while they can't move!" Furion's familiar nagging voice broke the silence. Riki snapped out of it. He jumped right into the time bubble. He moved incredibly fast, circling behind every single frozen enemy. He inflicted heavy, precise blows into their backs with Yasha. The exact moment he finished his final strike, the purple bubble disappeared. All five enemies collapsed to the ground, completely dead.

---

## Part 20 — Rampage

Suddenly, a deep, rumbling voice echoed from the sky. "Rampage," it roared. Riki thought it was just distant thunder, but the magical word made him feel incredibly powerful. With the defenders gone, the five teammates attacked the dark, fiery Ancient. They hit it with everything they had. The dark structure shook violently, cracked down the middle, and finally exploded into a pile of glowing rubble. The terrifying battle was finally over. They had won.

---

## Part 21 — Just the Beer

Instantly, another blinding flash of white light swallowed Riki. When he opened his eyes, he was back at his wooden table in the dim local bar. His friends were still laughing and drinking. Riki grabbed his head, groaning loudly. It had to be a crazy hallucination in his mind. "Are you okay, Riki? You look like you've seen a ghost," his closest friend asked, laughing. Riki sighed, convinced the dark beer had finally ruined his brain.

---

## Part 22 — The Package

Riki took another long gulp of his beer, desperately trying to forget the wild, terrifying trip. He finally started to calm down. Then, the heavy pub door slowly creaked open. A regular town courier walked straight to their table, dropped a wrapped package right next to Riki's mug, and quickly left. Riki froze, his heart pounding. Through a small tear in the brown paper, shiny steel reflected the pub lights. Carved into the handle was one word: Yasha.

## Story structure notes

Kept here so future edits do not accidentally break the chain.

- **Audience:** teenagers and up. Closer to a workplace sitcom than to the young
  origin stories. Cheeky staring in Part 9, one *hell*, people die. Nobody has sex. Do
  not sand it down into a children's file, and do not raise it toward
  `archive/queen-of-pain-mistress-by-mistake.md`.
- **One goal, stated early:** Part 6 says it out loud — destroy the enemy Ancient and
  the nightmare ends. Part 16 repeats it as the ticket home. Part 20 does it. Part 22
  asks whether any of it was a nightmare.
- **Riki's arc in one sentence:** a satyr who just wanted a quiet beer is dropped into
  a match he does not understand, learns it the hard way, and brings one piece of it
  home.
- **This is a match, told to someone who thinks it is a hallucination.** The reader
  who plays Dota should recognise the fountain, the stick, the creeps, the courier,
  Yasha, First Blood, the tree trap, the smoke, the time bubble, Rampage, and the
  fiery Ancient. Riki should not. Do not add a line that says "this is a Dota game".
- **Shape:** Parts 1–2 are the snatch. Parts 3–6 are the base (shop, team, quest,
  item). Parts 7–8 are the lane. Parts 9–15 are the sisters. Parts 16–20 are the
  Ancient. Parts 21–22 are the pub, and 22 is the only proof.
- **Setups and payoffs — do not remove these:**
  - **The wooden mug and the dark beer** (Part 1) → spiked beer as the explanation
    (Parts 2, 5, 21) → he drinks again, then the package lands next to the mug
    (Part 22). The beer is the fake cause. The mug is where the truth sits.
  - **White light swallows the pub** (Part 1) → white light swallows him home
    (Part 21). Same flash both ways. Do not change the colour.
  - **Not enough gold for a real sword** (Part 3) → he buys a stick (Part 6) → the
    stick is useless (Part 8) → he still will not walk back to the shop (Part 11) →
    the courier brings **Yasha** (Part 12) → Yasha kills the sisters (Part 15) and
    the five (Part 19) → Yasha is on the pub table (Part 22). The cheap start is why
    the ending lands.
  - **The paper: destroy the Ancient to go home** (Parts 5–6, 8, 16) → the Ancient
    falls (Part 20) → he is back in the pub (Part 21). Keep the paper's rule simple.
    Do not add extra quests.
  - **"creeps"** named because they creep (Part 7) → first kill, first guilt, first
    dagger (Part 8) → gold from dead creeps (Part 11). The joke is that they invent
    the real word.
  - **Riki wants to say hello to the sisters** (Part 10) → ice then fire → Furion
    yells **First Blood**. He never gets the joke. Keep him trying to be friendly
    once, and only once.
  - **Smoke bombs bought years ago for pub pranks** (Part 14) → the same pranks win
    the lane (Part 15). His old life is what saves him, not a new magic gift. Do
    not replace the pouches with a spell he finds on the map.
  - **Sisters die in the smoke** (Part 15) → wait at the Ancient, fully healed
    (Part 17). That is respawn, told as horror. Keep him baffled. Do not explain
    buyback or the graveyard.
  - **Faceless Void missing** (Part 17) → the purple bubble (Part 18) → Riki
    backstabs all five (Part 19) → **Rampage** (Part 20). Void has to be absent
    first or the bubble is just a trick, not a save.
  - **The flying donkey courier** (Part 12) → **a regular town courier** at the pub
    door (Part 22). Same job, two bodies. If you lose either courier, the last
    chapter stops making sense.
  - **"Just the beer"** (Part 21) is a lie the ending has to punch. Part 22 must
    still be the last line, and the last word must still be **Yasha**.
- **Canon notes for future edits:**
  - Riki is a satyr. Smoke Screen is a purple cloud that blinds. He hits from behind.
    Yasha is the speed item. Do not give him invisibility on the page unless a later
    edit is doing that on purpose — in this telling the smoke is the stealth.
  - Furion's tree rings are Sprout. Faceless Void's bubble is Chronosphere. The
    wooden branch is Iron Branch.
  - The glowing tree behind the fountain is the Radiant Ancient. The fiery enemy
    building is the Dire Ancient. Do not swap the two.
  - The old man with the bear is Lone Druid. The man with two wolves is Lycan. The
    sisters are Lina and Crystal Maiden. Leave them unnamed in the body.
  - A pub that dumps a satyr into a match is this story's invention. It is allowed
    to stay. Do not add a second frame story, a game client, or a "it was all a
    custom lobby" joke. The package is the only proof.
- **Never explain the ending.** Part 22 shows the handle and stops. Do not add a
  line that says it was real, or that it was not.
