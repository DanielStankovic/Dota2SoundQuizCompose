# Tinker and the Cupboard Kids

A fifteen-part story about Tinker, written for teenage readers. It is a school underdog comedy:
three boys nobody wants, one club room that used to be a cupboard, one goal written on the wall,
and one night on a hill when the whole school needs the three people it laughed at. Dry jokes for
most of it, then a long cold night, then an ending that is meant to feel earned.

Each part is roughly 100 words and is meant to be delivered one part per Hero Journey level
(parts 1–15 → levels 1–15).

Companion stories: `lina-golden-rolling-pin.md`, `juggernaut-sixty-one-carvings.md`,
`pudge-butcher-of-blackwater.md`.

Note for future agents: `pudge-butcher-of-blackwater.md` is also set in a school. The two stories
share nothing — different school, different cast, different genre (that one is horror, this one is
comedy). Never merge them and never move a character between them.

Note on sources: Liquipedia blocks the fetch tool with a human-verification page, so the hero
details here come from known Dota 2 canon (Tinker's instant rebuilding, Meepo's copies, Rubick's
lifting and copying, Windranger's speed, Legion Commander's duels), not from a live lore read.

## Localisation notes

**Hero names are NEVER translated.** Keep them exactly as written, in every language:

- Tinker
- Meepo
- Rubick
- Lyralei (the hero Windranger)
- Tresdin (the hero Legion Commander)

Note: Lyralei and Tresdin are called by their personal names, not by their hero names, because the
story is told from inside a school and that is what students would call each other. Both are canon
Dota names. Keep them. If a locale has an official transliteration of "Lyralei" or "Tresdin", use
it.

**"Legion"** — Tresdin's school nickname, and the one thing in this file a translator must get
right, because it is how the reader works out which hero she is. She is called **Legion**
everywhere in the story (Parts 4, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15) and **Tresdin** in only two places: the first
line of Part 5 and the last line of her scene in Part 15.

Part 5 spells out the chain: students started calling her *the legion commander* because she is
always in front of a crowd telling it where to go, that got shortened to *Lecom*, and that became
*Legion*. Use the locale's official wording for the hero name Legion Commander in the long form,
then shorten it the way that language would really shorten a nickname. The middle step *Lecom* may
be replaced with whatever short form sounds natural, and the final nickname may be a different
piece of the name, as long as a player hears Legion Commander in it. Keep it to one short word.
The joke only works if the shortening feels lazy and real.

Whatever short form you choose must then be used in every other part, because Part 15 depends on
it: she has been "Legion" to the reader for the whole story, and at the end she gives the three of
them her real name. Do not swap the two names around and do not use both in the same part.

Other do-not-translate names:

- **Ironcliff** — the school. Invented place name. Leave as-is or transliterate. Do not translate
  the meaning.

Items that *should* be translated, with care:

- **"the cupboard kids"** — the nickname the popular students use for the club. It appears in
  Parts 5, 9 and 15 and must be identical in all three. It is an insult, but a lazy one, the kind
  someone says while walking past. Translate it as "the kids from the broom cupboard" or whatever
  the locale calls the small room where a school keeps brooms and buckets. Do not use a word that
  means a nice small room, and do not make it sound clever.
- **"cupboard"** on its own (Parts 1, 8, 9, 12, 15) — the same word, used plainly. Room 12 really
  is a store cupboard with chairs in it. Use one word and keep it the same everywhere.
- **"losers"** (Part 5) and **"nerds"** (Part 9) — ordinary school insults. Use the everyday word
  teenagers in that language actually use. Do not soften them and do not make them aggressive.
  "Nerds" must keep its specific meaning: clever, into machines, no friends.
- **"the Machine Club"** (Part 1) — the club's official name. Translate the meaning. It should
  sound boring, because that is the joke.
- **"the Spring Dance"** (Parts 5, 9) — the school party. Translate the meaning. The same words
  both times.
- **"the Old Hall"** (Parts 5, 7, 10) — the building at the top of the hill. Translate the
  meaning, keep the capitals, and use the same words all three times.
- **"the hill lift"** (Parts 7, 11, 12) — two carts hanging from one steel cable, used to get up
  and down the hill. Use the plain everyday word in the target language for that machine. Do not
  use a technical engineering term.
- **"a duel"** (Parts 5, 15) — Tresdin challenges people to fight her one-on-one in front of
  everybody. If the locale has an official wording for Legion Commander's Duel, use it, so players
  recognise it. It must be the same word in both parts; Part 15 only works if the reader remembers
  Part 5. Together with the nickname, this is the second signal telling the reader who she is.
- All part titles.

Number note: the numbers are jokes and callbacks, not decoration. Keep them exactly as written —
121 students, 118 names, four chairs, three members, five Meepos, nine seconds, four sentences,
seven months, 40 years, forty metres, twelve people, nineteen tries, an hour and eleven minutes.
The pair 118 and 121 in Part 5 is the whole point of that part.

**Swearing — intentional, keep it mild.** There are two printed swears: *oh, shit* in Part 12 and
*oh fuck* in Part 13. Both are fear, not anger, and both land a punchline. The Part 13 one is the
stronger of the two on purpose, because that is the worse moment. Translate them with the everyday
casual equivalent in the target language — the level of "oh crap", not aggressive swearing. Do not
add more, do not make them stronger, and do not remove them.

### Style guidance — simple English is a hard rule

Most readers are **not native English speakers**, and the text will be machine-translated into
many languages. The English in this file is deliberately plain. Keep it that way when editing, and
match it when translating.

- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. Break long sentences in two.
- **Everyday words only.** The file says *cupboard*, not storeroom. *Lift*, not cable car system.
  *Fast*, not rapid. If a simpler word exists, it is the right word.
- **No literary or poetic language.** No long metaphors, no clever word order, no rare words.
- **Nothing the reader has to work out.** If a connection matters, the text says it directly.
- **Do not raise the register when translating.** Translators often make text more formal than the
  original. Do not. If your translation sounds like a school textbook, it is wrong.
- **Keep the deadpan.** The comedy works by reporting a sad or stupid thing calmly, with the worst
  detail saved for the last line of the part. Never signal the joke. No exclamation marks in the
  narration.
- **Almost no dialogue.** Keep it that way.

## Part 1 — Room 12

Ironcliff is a school on a hill. There are 121 students in the top year. Three of them meet in
Room 12.

Room 12 is a cupboard. It has a window the size of a book, a sink that does not work, and four
chairs. There have only ever been three members.

On paper it is the Machine Club. Nobody calls it that.

This year they wrote one goal on the wall. Not to win anything. Not to be good at anything. To be
invited to one party. Any party. Once.

In two years, one person has knocked on the door of Room 12. He was looking for the toilet.

---

## Part 2 — Tinker

Tinker is the smallest student at Ironcliff and the best in the school with machines. Only one of
those facts gets noticed.

He can take a broken thing and build it again out of the pieces, in seconds. He can do it over and
over. But it costs him. After five or six times he has to sit down on the floor, and his hands
shake, and he goes quiet.

Two years ago he fixed the school bell. He set it one second early, because he liked the number.
Nobody ever noticed.

He has never spoken to a girl for longer than nine seconds. He has counted.

---

## Part 3 — Meepo

Meepo is the second member. There are five of him.

He splits into copies. All five of them are him, which means all five do the homework, get five
different answers, and all five are wrong.

The club photo has seven people in it. The school thinks the club is bigger than it is. This is
the only advantage.

The copies share everything. When one is embarrassed, all five go red at the same time, in
different parts of the building.

Last term, one of him asked a girl to the dance. She said no. The other four already knew before
he got back.

---

## Part 4 — Rubick

Rubick is the third member. He can do two things.

He can lift something without touching it. He uses this on other people's lunch, from across the
room. No teacher has ever caught him. The owner of the lunch always does.

And he can copy any trick he sees. Exactly once, exactly right, always at the wrong moment. Last
year he copied Legion's walk and did it behind her back. She was standing in front of a window.

He also has his own laugh. It is loud and it goes up at the end, and the whole year does an
impression of it.

Nobody does it kindly.

---

## Part 5 — The List

Her name is Tresdin. Nobody uses it. She was captain of one team in her first year and six by this
one, always in front of a crowd, telling it where to go. So people called her the legion
commander. That became Lecom, and then Legion.

Legion never argues. If she thinks you are wrong she offers you a duel, in front of everybody,
until one gives up. Nine duels, nine wins.

In April she put the Spring Dance list on the noticeboard, for the dance in the Old Hall at the
top of the hill.

The list had 118 names. The year has 121 students.

Walking past it she called them the cupboard kids, and then *losers*.

---

## Part 6 — Nine Seconds

Lyralei is the fastest person at Ironcliff. She runs the hill road in the dark for fun. When she
really runs, the air moves with her and the papers come off the noticeboard.

Tinker planned four sentences. He wrote them down. He practised them in the cupboard, out loud, to
Meepo, which is practising to five people.

On Thursday she stopped at his desk and asked him something normal.

He told her the school bell was one second early.

Then he stood up, said nothing else, and walked into a door.

Nine seconds. He counted them, because he always does.

---

## Part 7 — The Lift

The Old Hall sits at the top of the hill. The only way up is the hill lift: two carts hanging from
one steel cable, built 40 years ago, back when the hill had a mine in it.

The school engineer retired in the spring. Nobody replaced him.

So for two years, on Saturday mornings, Tinker has walked up to the lift house on his own. He oils
the motor. He checks the brake. He tightens the bolts on the cable. Nobody asked him to, and
nobody told him to stop, because nobody knew.

He keeps a notebook of every part in that machine.

---

## Part 8 — The Roof

In May somebody took Tinker's machine apart and put the pieces on the roof of the sports hall.

It was not a dangerous machine. It was a small walking thing with six legs that carried his bag to
class. It had taken him seven months.

Rubick lifted the pieces down one at a time, without touching them. It took until dark. Meepo
carried them, all five of him, in five trips.

Tinker built it again in under a minute. That is what he can do.

Then he sat down on the floor of the cupboard, and his hands shook, and he said nothing for a long
time.

---

## Part 9 — The Joke

The next morning, Legion added three names to the list.

Everybody understood it straight away. The cupboard kids were invited because three nerds standing
alone at a dance is funny, and a dance needs something funny in it.

The club understood it too. They talked about it for one afternoon in Room 12.

They went anyway.

Rubick ironed a shirt for the first time in his life and burnt a mark into it the shape of the
iron. Meepo came as five people, in five suits, borrowed from five different houses.

Tinker brought the four sentences, folded up, in his pocket.

---

## Part 10 — The Dance

The Old Hall is big and warm and loud, and they stood next to the food for two hours.

That is not a joke about them. The food table is the only place in a room like that where standing
still looks normal.

Lyralei danced. Not with Tinker. He worked out that he would have to cross about eleven metres of
floor, and he did not cross any of them.

At ten o'clock, some of Legion's friends asked all five Meepos to do something funny. All five
said no, at the same time, in the same voice. The friends went away.

Then the rain started.

---

## Part 11 — The Storm

The storm came over the hill all at once, the way they do there.

The dance was stopped early. The whole top year had to get down, and the lift takes twelve people
at a time, so they went down in groups.

The fourth cart went out over the valley. Halfway across, the motor in the lift house burnt, and
the noise it made was not a loud noise. It was a small one.

The cart stopped. Then it slid backwards half a metre and stopped again, swinging, forty metres
above the river, in the rain, in the dark.

Legion was inside it.

So was the last teacher left on the hill.

---

## Part 12 — The Lift House

Inside the lift house there is a hand brake. It is the thing that stops the cable running free. It
was built for two grown men to pull.

It was slipping. You could hear it slipping.

Somebody looked at the burnt motor and said *oh, shit*, quietly, which was the correct answer.

Tinker opened his notebook. He already knew what was wrong. Four parts of that motor always burn,
and he had written down the order to fix them two years ago, on a Saturday, for no reason at all.

Fixing it would take him about forty seconds.

It would also take about nineteen tries.

---

## Part 13 — The Brake

Meepo took the brake. All five of him, hands over hands, feet against the wall.

Five of him is not five strong people. It is one worried boy, five times. They held it anyway, and
when the cable pulled, all five said *oh fuck* at exactly the same moment.

Rubick went out into the rain and lifted Tinker twenty metres up the cable tower, without touching
him. The heaviest thing he had ever lifted before was a lunch tray. He held his friend up there
for an hour and eleven minutes and never let him drop once.

For the first time in his life, he used it at the right moment.

---

## Part 14 — Nineteen Times

Tinker rebuilt that motor nineteen times.

Each time the pieces came back together, the cable moved the cart a few metres, and then something
burnt again, and he did it again. By the sixth time his hands were shaking. By the twelfth he
could not feel them. He held the notebook open with his knee.

Lyralei ran down the hill road in the dark to fetch the old engineer. She ran it faster than
anybody has ever run it. It made no difference, because it was finished before she got back.

Twelve people stepped out of that cart at midnight. One of them was Legion.

---

## Part 15 — The Fourth Chair

On Monday, Room 12 was full.

They get invited to everything now. Meepo gets five of every invitation, and nobody can stop it.

Legion came down to the cupboard and offered Tinker a duel. It is the only way she knows to say
she was wrong. He said no, thank you. So she used their names instead, all three, and got them
right. Then she told them hers was Tresdin.

The whole year still does Rubick's laugh. Now they do it with him.

And the fourth chair finally has somebody in it. Lyralei sits in it on Tuesdays.

Tinker used one of the four sentences on her. It was the wrong one.

She said yes anyway.

## Story structure notes

Kept here so future edits do not accidentally break the chain.

- **Audience:** teenagers. School underdog comedy, the kind everyone has seen in a film. The
  reader should know roughly where it is going and enjoy getting there. The surprise is never the
  plot; it is always the detail.
- **One goal, stated early:** Part 1 writes it on the wall. Get invited to one party, once. Part 9
  grants the goal in the cruellest possible way (invited as the joke of the evening), and Part 15
  pays it properly. Do not move the goal statement out of Part 1.
- **Tinker's arc in one sentence:** the boy nobody looked at had been looking after everybody for
  two years already. He does not change in this story. The school does.
- **Tone turn:** Parts 1–6 are introductions and jokes. Parts 7–8 plant the machine and land the
  low point. Parts 9–10 are the humiliation. Part 11 is where the comedy stops. Parts 12–14 are
  the long night. Part 15 lands the heart.
- **Early parts stand alone:** Parts 2, 3 and 4 are each one character and one joke, because
  levels 1–6 are where readers drop off. Each ends on its own punchline and can be read on its
  own.
- **Cliffhangers that pull the reader to the next level:** the visitor looking for the toilet (1),
  nine seconds (2), the other four already knew (3), nobody does it kindly (4), *losers* (5),
  walking into a door (6), the notebook (7), his hands shake (8), the four sentences in his pocket
  (9), the rain starts (10), Legion is inside the cart (11), nineteen tries (12), the right moment
  (13), twelve people out at midnight (14).
- **Setups and payoffs — do not remove these:**
  - **Four chairs, three members** (Part 1) → the fourth chair finally has somebody in it, and it
    is Lyralei (Part 15). **This is the ending of the whole story.** Everything else exists so that
    the last image is a chair that is no longer empty. Never change the chair count in Part 1.
  - **The four sentences.** Tinker writes four sentences to say to Lyralei and practises them
    (Part 6) → he uses none of them and walks into a door (Part 6) → he carries them folded in his
    pocket to the dance and still says nothing (Part 9) → he finally uses one, and it is the wrong
    one, and she says yes (Part 15). The last three lines of the story are the payoff of Part 6.
    Do not let him succeed earlier.
  - **Nine seconds** (Part 2) → the conversation in Part 6 is exactly nine seconds long. Keep both
    numbers.
  - **The school bell, set one second early, that nobody noticed** (Part 2) → it is the thing he
    blurts out to Lyralei (Part 6). It is also the small version of the whole story: he fixes
    things for people who never find out.
  - **Tinker's rebuilding costs him.** Stated in Part 2 (after five or six times he sits down and
    his hands shake) → shown in Part 8 after one rebuild of his own machine → paid in Part 14,
    where he does it nineteen times. Part 2 and Part 8 exist so the reader knows what nineteen
    costs. Without them, Part 14 is just a number.
  - **The lift.** Tinker secretly maintains it on Saturdays and keeps a notebook of every part
    (Part 7) → the notebook already contains the exact repair order, written two years earlier for
    no reason (Part 12) → he holds it open with his knee while his hands fail (Part 14). Part 7
    must stay in the first half, or the rescue looks like luck instead of two years of unnoticed
    work.
  - **Rubick lifts things without touching them, and only ever lunch** (Part 4) → he lifts the
    machine parts off the roof (Part 8) → he holds Tinker up a tower for an hour and eleven
    minutes (Part 13). The line "the heaviest thing he had ever lifted before was a lunch tray"
    only works if Part 4 stays.
  - **Rubick copies tricks "always at the wrong moment"** (Part 4) → "for the first time in his
    life, he used it at the right moment" (Part 13). Same idea, turned around. Keep the wording
    close.
  - **Rubick's laugh, which the whole year imitates unkindly** (Part 4) → they still do it, but
    now they do it with him (Part 15). One line, do not expand it.
  - **Five Meepos are a weakness, then a strength.** Five rejections from one girl (Part 3), five
    borrowed suits (Part 9), five voices refusing to be the entertainment (Part 10) → five of him
    on the brake, and the honest line that five of him is one worried boy five times (Part 13) →
    five of every invitation (Part 15). The joke never changes; only what it is worth changes.
  - **Legion's duel.** She settles everything with a duel and has never lost (Part 5) → she offers
    Tinker one, because it is the only apology she has (Part 15). He declines, and she uses their
    names instead. She must never actually say sorry.
  - **Her two names.** Part 5 opens with "Her name is Tresdin. Nobody uses it", explains how the
    school turned *the legion commander* into *Lecom* and then *Legion*, and from that point the
    story calls her Legion and nothing else → in Part 15 she gives the three of them her real
    name, which is the same gift she just gave them by using theirs. The nickname chain exists for
    two reasons and both matter: it tells a Dota player which hero she is, and it sets up that
    last line. Never use "Tresdin" anywhere between Part 5 and Part 15.
  - **118 and 121** (Part 5) → three names added as a joke (Part 9). The reader must be able to do
    that subtraction instantly. Do not change either number.
  - **"The cupboard kids"** (Parts 5, 9) → Legion comes down to the cupboard herself in Part 15.
    The word has to appear as an insult twice before the last part, or the visit means nothing.
  - **The Old Hall and the hill** are introduced as the place they are not invited to (Part 5),
    then as the place they are (Parts 9–10), then as the place everybody has to get down from
    (Part 11).
- **The sincere beats are the end of Part 8, Parts 13–14, and Part 15, and they must stay
  sincere.** Part 15 keeps two small jokes at the top (the five invitations, the declined duel)
  and then stops being funny. No jokes in the fourth chair or in the last three lines.
- **Nobody is punished.** Legion is never humiliated, the bullies are never named and never pay
  for it, and the club never takes revenge. The story is about being seen, not about winning.
  Do not add a scene where someone gets what they deserve.
- **Never explain the ending.** Part 15 states the facts and stops. Do not add a line telling the
  reader how to feel about the chair.
