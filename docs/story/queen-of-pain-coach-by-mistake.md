A fifteen-part story about Queen of Pain (Akasha), told in simple English. It is a comedy about a
demon who cannot hold down a normal job: she is too loud, too rude and too angry for any workplace
in Amsterdam, until she accidentally walks into the one job where being loud, rude and angry is
the product.

Each part is roughly 100 words and is meant to be delivered one part per Hero Journey level
(parts 1–15 → levels 1–15).

Companion stories: `lina-golden-rolling-pin.md`, `juggernaut-sixty-one-carvings.md`,
`pudge-butcher-of-blackwater.md`, `sniper-eleven-bullets.md`,
`tinker-the-cupboard-kids.md`, `invoker-three-wisps.md`.

## Localisation notes

**Hero names are NEVER translated.** Keep them exactly as written, in every language:

- Akasha (the hero Queen of Pain)
- Davion (the hero Dragon Knight)
- Kunkka
- Chen

Note: Akasha and Davion are called by their personal names, not their hero names, because the
story is set in the ordinary world where people have ordinary names. Both are canon Dota names.
Keep them. If a locale has an official transliteration of "Akasha" or "Davion", use it.

Items that *should* be translated, with care:

- **"the Queen of Pain"** — the last line of the story and the whole point of the file. **Use the
  official in-game localised version of the hero name Queen of Pain**, exactly, so the player
  recognises it. The sentence must read as a title that other people gave her, not as a
  description. If the official hero name is awkward inside a sentence in that locale, change the
  sentence around it — never change the name.
- **"scream of pain"** (Parts 9, 14, and the Part 9 title "The Scream of Pain") and **"sonic
  wave"** (Part 9) — these are two of Queen of Pain's real abilities, hidden in plain sight.
  **Use the official in-game localised names for both.** In the body text they are written in
  lower case so they read as plain description; keep them lower case there, and keep the capitals
  in the part title. A player should feel the connection without the story pointing at it.
- **"Coach"** (Parts 5, 6, and in the story title) — what clients call Akasha. Use the word
  people in that language actually shout at a gym trainer, not a formal job title and not a
  sports-team manager. Capital C when a client says it, and identical in all three places.
- **"Coach by Mistake"** — the story title. The joke is that she got the job by accident. Keep
  that meaning, and **write new wordplay if the sound of the English does not survive.** A title
  that works beats a literal translation.
- **"The Demon of Amsterdam"** — the second half of the title. Translate "the Demon of" and keep
  "Amsterdam".
- **Amsterdam** — real city. Use the locale's normal name for it. This is the only setting in the
  folder in a real place, and that is deliberate; do not move it to a Dota location.
- **"The Pit"** — the gym. A business name, in single quotes in Parts 3 and 14. Leave it in
  English, the way a real Amsterdam gym with an English name would be written. Do not translate
  the meaning and do not add an explanation.
- **"boot camp gym"** (Part 3) — a gym where a trainer shouts at people like an army instructor.
  Use the everyday words for that in the target language.
- **"euros"** (Parts 2, 6, 7) — the story is set in Amsterdam, so the currency stays euros in
  every language. Do not convert the amounts. Ten euros, two hundred euros.
- **"Cleaning lady needed. High pay."** (Part 3) — the sign on the door. Short and plain, the way
  a real handwritten sign reads.
- **"Pay up or get out."** (Part 2) — the landlord's final letter. It must sound like a threat
  from a real letter, not like legal language.
- **"Davion highly recommends the angry woman with the rope."** and **"Kunkka loved the
  terrifying sonic screams."** (Part 10) — two online reviews. Keep them short and slightly badly
  written, the way real reviews are. The names stay.
- All part titles.

### The insults and the idioms

- **"You stupid, pathetic little pig!"** (Part 9) — the loudest line in the story. Use insults a
  furious person in that language would really shout. Do not soften them. "Pig" must stay an
  animal insult, because the client then agrees that he is one.
- **"she told customers they looked fat"** (Part 2) — keep it blunt. This is the line that proves
  she cannot do normal jobs.
- **"like proud lions"** (Part 3) → **"crawled at her feet like bugs"** (Part 14). These two are
  a matched pair. Keep both as animal comparisons so the reversal is visible.
- **"Beggars couldn't be choosers."** (Part 3) — a proverb. **Replace it with the equivalent
  proverb in the target language.** Do not translate the words.
- **"It felt like a cheat code for life."** (Part 7) — a gaming expression. Keep it if gamers in
  that language say it; otherwise use their everyday phrase for getting something far too easily.
- **"Her blood boiled."** (Part 8) and **"Time is money!"** (Part 5) — common idioms. Use the
  locale's normal equivalent, not a literal translation.
- **"pay top euro"** (Part 12) — an expression meaning "pay a very high price". Use the locale's
  normal phrase for that, built on the same currency. Do not translate it word for word.
- **"Smack!"** (Part 6) — the sound of the rope hitting the floor. Use the sound word that
  language actually writes in comics. Keep the exclamation mark; it is the only one allowed in
  the narration.

**Swearing — deliberately mild, and it must stay that way.** There are exactly two soft words,
*damn* in Part 1 and *hell* in Part 5. This file exists because the stronger version of this
story is not safe to ship, so **do not strengthen them in any language** and do not add new ones.
Akasha is frightening because of her volume, not her vocabulary.

### Style guidance — simple English is a hard rule

Most readers are **not native English speakers**, and the text will be machine-translated into
many languages. The English in this file is deliberately plain. Keep it that way when editing, and
match it when translating.

- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. Break long sentences in two.
- **Everyday words only.** The file says *angry*, not enraged. *Job*, not position. If a simpler
  word exists, it is the right word.
- **Do not raise the register when translating.** This is a story about a rude woman in a cheap
  job. If your translation sounds polite, it is wrong.
- **Nothing sexual, in any language.** The clients want to be shouted at and worked until they
  cry. They are paying for brutal training and nothing else. Do not add innuendo, and do not let
  "Destroy me" (Part 5) or "Yes! More!" (Part 6) drift into a sexual reading in translation — if
  either reads that way in the target language, replace it with plain gym language.
- **Keep the deadpan.** The comedy works by reporting absurd things flatly, with the best detail
  last. Never signal the joke.

## Part 1 — The Fired Demon

Akasha was fired again. She worked as an office secretary, but her loud, aggressive voice always scared her boss. She walked out into the cold Amsterdam streets. She had no money for rent or food, and the electric bill was past due. She looked at her reflection in a shop window. She was beautiful, with sharp features and piercing eyes, but her good looks didn't pay the bills. "Damn this city," she muttered. She needed a new job fast, or she would be sleeping on the streets. The normal world was just not for her.

---

## Part 2 — Failing Hard

She tried everything over the next few weeks. Akasha worked at a bakery, but she burned the bread. She worked as a waitress, but she dropped plates when a customer was rude to her. She even tried selling clothes, but she told customers they looked fat. That got her fired in two hours. Her mailbox was full of angry letters from her landlord. "Pay up or get out," the final letter said. Akasha felt hopeless. She had exactly ten euros left. Her stomach growled. She needed a miracle, or at least a very stupid boss.

---

## Part 3 — The Glass Gyms

Akasha walked home past the luxury gyms every night. Behind the bright glass, trainers with perfect teeth smiled at rich men and told them they were doing great. She hated it. The whole street disgusted her. "I would never do that," she always told herself. "Lying to people for money is pathetic." But tonight, even lying looked like a job. She was desperate. She saw a sign on a dark, hidden door in an alley. It belonged to a hardcore boot camp gym called 'The Pit'. The sign read: "Cleaning lady needed. High pay." Beggars couldn't be choosers.

---

## Part 4 — The Pit Cleaner

The gym owner was a scheming, business-like man named Chen. He was a hypocrite who talked about holy discipline and clean living while eating fried food and counting his money in the back room. Chen hired her immediately because she was cheap. Akasha became the new cleaning lady. The pay was decent, but the job was a nightmare. She cleaned up chalk dust, broken glass, and sticky things she didn't want to name. "These people are sick," she thought, vigorously scrubbing the floor. The gym felt strange and terrible to her, but her first paycheck stopped her landlord's threats.

---

## Part 5 — The Wrong Studio

On a busy Friday night, Chen gave her a quick order. "Clean studio four, now! Time is money!" Akasha grabbed her mop and bucket. She opened the door, expecting an empty room. Instead, she found a huge muscular man holding a deep squat against the wall, waiting in the dark. He was shaking with effort. Akasha froze. She stepped back, but accidentally tripped over a heavy skipping rope on the floor. She picked it up, frustrated and angry. "What the hell is this mess?" she snapped. The man looked at her with excited eyes. "Yes, Coach," he whispered. "Destroy me."

---

## Part 6 — The First Round

Akasha was confused. She just wanted to clean the stupid room! In a moment of pure annoyance, she cracked the skipping rope against the floor. Smack! The man dropped and started doing push-ups. "Yes! More!" he gasped. Akasha blinked in shock. "Are you crazy?" she asked. She shouted at him to go faster, purely out of frustration. The man worked even harder. When his session time ended, he gave her two hundred euros in cash. "Thank you, Coach. My name is Davion," he bowed respectfully. Akasha stood alone with the mop in one hand and cash in the other.

---

## Part 7 — Easy Money

Akasha walked home with a fat wallet. Davion thought she was a real trainer, but she was wearing dirty cleaning clothes! It was hilarious. The next morning, she paid her rent. She paid her electric bill. She even bought an expensive coffee. "Two hundred euros just to shout at an idiot doing push-ups," she thought, sipping her drink. It felt like a cheat code for life. She didn't have to be nice to anyone, she just had to be mean. Still, she planned to stay quiet and stick to her mop. One crazy guy was enough.

---

## Part 8 — A New Mess

A few days passed normally. Akasha kept cleaning the dark, sticky floors of the gym. Then, on Tuesday, she opened the door to studio seven. A tall man was inside, throwing weights down and spilling his drink all over the expensive rubber floor. He was a rich, arrogant businessman paying for a tough love session, but Akasha didn't know that. She only saw her clean floor being ruined. Her blood boiled. She dropped her bucket. All her anger from losing her past jobs, the unpaid bills, and this disgusting gym rushed into her chest. She took a deep breath.

---

## Part 9 — The Scream of Pain

"What do you think you are doing?!" Akasha screamed at the top of her lungs. Her loud voice echoed like a sonic wave in the small room. It was literally a scream of pain that physically hurt his ears. "You stupid, pathetic little pig! I just cleaned this floor!" She yelled terrible insults at him. The businessman fell to his knees. He started crying, but he had a huge smile on his face. "Yes, I am a pig! I am so sorry!" he begged. He handed her a giant tip. Akasha stopped yelling. This was getting absolutely ridiculous.

---

## Part 10 — The Reputation Grows

Word travels fast in a small gym. The website got two new five-star reviews. One read: "Davion highly recommends the angry woman with the rope." The other read: "Kunkka loved the terrifying sonic screams." Chen, the scheming owner, called Akasha into his office. "Who is paying you?" he asked, counting his coins. "Nobody!" Akasha defended herself. "I shouted at one guy and screamed at another!" Chen laughed loudly. "They loved it! You have a natural talent for this. My clients are begging for you. I want to offer you a highly profitable position."

---

## Part 11 — The Offer

Chen pushed a contract across his messy desk. The salary was ten times higher than her cleaning job. Akasha stared at the paper. For days, she thought about it. She hated the whole fitness business. She always promised herself she would never lie to people for money like the smiling trainers behind the glass. But this was different. These men did not want to be told they were great. They wanted to be broken. She already hated people anyway, so why not get paid for it? She went back to Chen's office with some strict demands.

---

## Part 12 — Setting the Rules

"I will take the job," Akasha told Chen, pointing a warning finger at him. "But I have rules. I will never lie to a client. I will never say well done. No fake smiling, ever." Chen nodded eagerly, rubbing his hands together. "What exactly will you do then?" he asked. Akasha smiled, a wicked look in her beautiful eyes. "I will scream at them. I will break them. I will make them cry. And they will thank me for it." Chen clapped. "Perfect! Clients will pay top euro for a real monster." Akasha was finally in charge.

---

## Part 13 — The Costume

A professional worker needs a good outfit. The gym bought Akasha a custom costume. It was tight black training gear with silver spikes on the shoulders. To make it special and theatrical, they added a pair of fake, dark bat wings on the back and a thin, pointed devil tail. When Akasha looked in the mirror, she looked like a stunning demon. Her natural beauty combined with the terrifying outfit made her look dangerous. She grabbed a fresh, heavy skipping rope and snapped it in the air. The loud crack echoed through the changing room. She was ready.

---

## Part 14 — The Queen is Born

Her first official night was a massive success. Men lined up outside 'The Pit' just to see her. Akasha worked them until they cried. She used her incredibly loud voice to scream insults, breaking their pride into tiny pieces with her famous scream of pain. And the crazy part? They paid her huge amounts of cash for it. The same men who walked into those glass gyms like proud lions now crawled at her feet like bugs. She wasn't disgusted by the gym anymore. She was the boss of it. She didn't clean the floors; she made men lick them clean.

---

## Part 15 — A Legend in Amsterdam

Months later, Akasha never worried about unpaid bills again. She lived in a luxury apartment and drank expensive wine. She never once told a client they were doing great, but she became the most famous trainer in the city. The men didn't even know her real name anymore. Because of her leather bat wings, her cruel rope, and her terrifying screams, they gave her a new title. Whenever men whispered about the hardest coach in Amsterdam, they called her the Queen of Pain. Akasha smiled as she counted her cash. Sometimes, failing at normal jobs was the best thing ever.

## Story structure notes

Kept here so future edits do not accidentally break the chain.

- **Why this file exists.** `QOP-A` puts the same story in a BDSM club in the Red Light District,
  which is not safe to publish on an app store. This version changes the **venue and nothing
  else**: a hardcore boot camp gym where rich soft men pay to be screamed at and worked until
  they cry. That is an ordinary, legal, widely sold product, so the premise survives intact
  without any adult content. Every joke, every number and every payoff is in the same part as in
  `QOP-A`.
- **The four things that changed, and nothing else should:**
  1. The club became a gym, the whip became a heavy skipping rope, and "Mistress" became "Coach".
  2. Nobody enjoys being hit. The clients enjoy being *trained past their limit*. Part 6 is the
     key one: she cracks the rope **on the floor**, not on a person, and he responds by doing
     push-ups. Keep it that way.
  3. Her promise in Part 3 changed from refusing sex work to refusing to lie for money, and her
     rules in Part 12 changed to match. The shape is identical: she makes a promise before she is
     desperate and keeps it to the end.
  4. The two f-words became *damn* and *hell*.
- **One goal, stated early:** Part 1 says it plainly — she needs money or she sleeps on the
  street. Every part until Part 7 is about money, and Part 15 closes it with "never worried about
  unpaid bills again". Do not replace the money goal with a self-respect goal; the self-respect
  arrives on its own, which is the point.
- **Akasha's arc in one sentence:** the thing that got her fired from every job in the city turns
  out to be worth money. She never becomes a nicer person and she must not.
- **This is an origin story, and the reveal is for the player, not for Akasha.** She is never
  called Queen of Pain until other people name her in the last two lines. The clues are planted
  earlier and must stay: "The Fired Demon" (Part 1 title), the scream (Part 9), the wings and
  tail (Part 13), "The Queen is Born" (Part 14 title). The name itself lands once, at the end.
- **The line the whole story depends on is in Part 3:** *"Lying to people for money is
  pathetic."* She says it before she is desperate, she turns it into her working rules in Part 12,
  and Part 15 confirms she never broke it — "She never once told a client they were doing great."
  Those three lines are one chain. Never cut any of them.
- **Setups and payoffs — do not remove these:**
  - **Her voice gets her fired** (Part 1: "her loud, aggressive voice always scared her boss") →
    the voice is the accident that makes her famous (Part 9) → it is her product (Part 14) → it
    is half of why they crown her (Part 15). This is the spine of the story. Part 1 must keep the
    word "loud".
  - **The rope she trips over by accident** (Part 5) → her first two hundred euros (Part 6) → a
    stranger's online review (Part 10) → a new heavy one bought for her (Part 13) → "her cruel
    rope" in the final list (Part 15).
  - **Cleaning the floors.** She scrubs them (Part 4), she rages because a client ruined a clean
    floor (Part 8 — her anger is about the floor, not about him, and that must stay) → "She
    didn't clean the floors; she made men lick them clean" (Part 14). That sentence is the best
    line in the story.
  - **"like proud lions"** (Part 3) → **"crawled at her feet like bugs"** (Part 14). Same men,
    smaller animal.
  - **Chen counts his money** (Part 4) and counts his coins while accusing her (Part 10) →
    **she** counts her cash in the last line but one (Part 15). He never changes and she takes
    his gesture.
  - **The unpaid bills.** No money for rent or food and an overdue electric bill (Part 1), ten
    euros left and a landlord's final letter (Part 2) → the first paycheck stops the threats
    (Part 4) → she pays rent and the electric bill and buys one expensive coffee (Part 7) → the
    luxury apartment (Part 15). The coffee is the detail that sells it; keep it.
  - **The smiling trainers behind the glass** (Part 3) → the thing she refuses to become
    (Part 11) → her rules (Part 12) → the last proof that she never became one (Part 15).
  - **Davion is the accident and Kunkka is the proof.** Davion gives her the job by mistake
    (Parts 5–6), and the two reviews in Part 10 are what turn one accident into a career. Both
    men must stay named, because the reviews are funnier with names on them.
  - **The costume is bought by the gym as theatre** (Part 13) and is the reason for the title
    (Part 15). Nobody in the story ever suspects she is really a demon. Keep it that way — the
    joke is that the fake wings are not fake.
- **Cliffhangers that pull the reader to the next level:** she needs a job or the street (1), ten
  euros left (2), the sign on the door (3), the job is a nightmare but the rent is paid (4), "Yes,
  Coach" (5), cash in one hand and a mop in the other (6), one crazy guy was enough (7), she
  takes a deep breath (8), this was getting ridiculous (9), a highly profitable position (10),
  she has demands (11), she is finally in charge (12), she was ready (13), she made men lick the
  floors (14).
- **Nobody is rescued and nobody is punished.** Chen stays a hypocrite, the clients stay
  ridiculous, and Akasha stays rude. There is no moral at the end and none should be added. The
  last line is a shrug, and it must stay a shrug.
