# Invoker and the Three Wisps

A fifteen-part story about Invoker (Kael), told in simple English for a young audience. It is a
**fantasy quest**: a boy with no magic at all is sent to save a realm of wizards, and finds three
quarrelling wisps who have all the power and none of the sense. Light adventure, three comic
voices, and an origin: by the last part the reader has watched Kael become the hero they play in
the game.

Each part is roughly 80 words and is meant to be delivered one part per Hero Journey level
(parts 1–15 → levels 1–15). **These parts are shorter than the other stories in this folder**
(80 instead of 100) — that is how the story was written and it is deliberate. If parts are ever
expanded, expand all fifteen, not some.

Companion stories: `lina-golden-rolling-pin.md`, `juggernaut-sixty-one-carvings.md`,
`pudge-butcher-of-blackwater.md`, `sniper-eleven-bullets.md`,
`tinker-the-cupboard-kids.md`.

Note for future agents: Invoker also appears as a **side character** (a teacher) in
`pudge-butcher-of-blackwater.md`. That is a different story in a different world and the two
share nothing. This file is the only story where Invoker is the hero. Do not merge them and do
not carry anything from one into the other.

## Localisation notes

**Hero names are NEVER translated.** Keep them exactly as written, in every language:

- Invoker
- Kael
- Quas
- Wex
- Exort

Notes on these five:

- **Kael** is Invoker's canon personal name. The story never calls him Invoker, on purpose — the
  title does that job. Keep "Kael" everywhere. If a locale has an official transliteration of
  "Kael", use it.
- **Quas, Wex and Exort** are the official names of Invoker's three orbs in the game. They are
  names, not words, and they are **never translated and never transliterated** — write them in
  the Latin alphabet exactly as they appear here, in every locale, including languages that do
  not use the Latin alphabet. Players must recognise these three words on sight. Never replace
  them with their meanings (ice, lightning, fire), and never add the meaning in brackets.

Other do-not-translate names:

- **Ultimyr** — the realm the story starts and ends in. This is a canon Dota name (the Ultimyr
  Academy). Translate **only** if the game already has an official translation in that locale;
  otherwise leave as-is or transliterate. Do not translate the meaning.

Items that *should* be translated, with care:

- **The three spell names — "Ice Wall" (Part 11), "Chaos Meteor" (Part 13) and "Deafening Blast"
  (Part 14).** These are real Invoker spells. **Use the official in-game localised name for each
  one**, exactly, so players recognise them. This is the single most important instruction in this
  file: the whole last third of the story is a player seeing spells they know being cast for the
  first time. Each name appears twice — once as the part title and once shouted inside the part —
  and both must be identical.
- **Sunstone** — the magic stone Kael is sent to fetch. Invented name, and the goal of the whole
  story. Translate the meaning (sun + stone), keep the capital letter, and use the same word in
  Parts 2, 10, 15 and the Part 15 title.
- **Whispering Woods** and **Scorched Desert** — invented place names, but both describe the
  place rather than naming a town, so translate the meaning and keep the capitals. Use the same
  words every time each one appears.
- **"the High Council"**, **"the Headmaster"**, **"the elder wizards"** — ordinary titles.
  Translate normally with everyday words. Do not use grand or official-sounding vocabulary.
- **"wisp"** — what Quas, Wex and Exort are. Use the plainest word in the target language for a
  small floating ball of light. Do not use a technical or folklore-specific term that readers
  would have to look up.
- All part titles.

### The insults and nicknames — adapt these, do not translate them literally

The three wisps insult each other constantly. That bickering is the comedy of the story, so these
must land as real teasing in the target language. Where a literal translation would be flat or
strange, **write a new insult that works** — it only has to be short, rude in a friendly way, and
based on the same idea.

- **"book-boy"** — Exort to Kael, Part 4 and Part 12. A callback: it is the first thing anyone
  says about Kael and it comes back when he is about to be brave. Keep the two identical.
- **"hothead"** — Wex and Quas to Exort, Parts 6 and 11. A pun: it means both "made of fire" and
  "angry person". If the target language has a word that does both, use it. If not, pick the
  "angry" meaning, because that is the joke.
- **"sparky"** (Part 7), **"ice-cube"** (Part 8), **"purple boy"** (Part 13), **"slowpokes"**
  (Part 6), **"overgrown rock"** (Part 12), **"ugly dog"** (Part 4) — ordinary playground
  insults. Use whatever a child in that language would actually say.
- **"Don't freeze up now"** (Part 12) — this means "do not stop moving because you are scared".
  It is also a small joke, because one of his friends is made of ice. Translate the meaning
  first; keep the ice joke only if it is free.
- **"Why do I have to be the puddle?"** (Part 11) — Exort complaining that his fire has been
  reduced to melting a thin layer of ice so the goblins stick to the ground. The joke is that the
  fire wisp has been given the least impressive job in the spell. If "puddle" is not funny in the
  target language, write a different short complaint with the same meaning.

**Swearing — there is none in this story, on purpose.** This is the young-audience end of the
folder. Do not add any, in any language, including in the insults above.

### Style guidance — simple English is a hard rule

Most readers are **not native English speakers**, and the text will be machine-translated into
many languages. The English in this file is deliberately plain. Keep it that way when editing, and
match it when translating.

- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. Break long sentences in two.
- **Everyday words only.** The file says *scary*, not terrifying. *Boy*, not youth. If a simpler
  word exists, it is the right word.
- **No literary or poetic language.** No long metaphors, no clever word order, no rare words.
- **Do not raise the register when translating.** Translators often make text more formal than the
  original. Do not. If your translation sounds like a school textbook, it is wrong.
- **This story uses dialogue, and that is an exception in this folder.** The other stories are
  almost entirely narrated. Here the three wisps talk constantly, because their arguing is the
  comedy. Keep the dialogue. Do not convert it to narration and do not add more of it.
- **The three voices must stay different, and this matters more than word-for-word accuracy.**
  Exort is loud, keen and angry, and he shouts. Wex is fast, teasing and cheerful, and he never
  stops moving. Quas is slow, deep and short — he almost never uses more than four words. If a
  reader cannot tell which wisp is speaking without the name, the translation has failed.

## Part 1 — The Boy With No Spark

In the high realm of Ultimyr, everyone was a master of magic. Everyone except young Kael. Despite coming from the greatest family of wizards, Kael had no power. He read every spellbook and mixed endless potions, but not a single spark left his fingers. The other children laughed at him. Kael felt broken and useless. He spent his days hiding behind giant piles of books, wishing he could cast just one simple spell to make his family proud.

---

## Part 2 — The Fog

One morning, a dark, magic-eating fog covered Ultimyr. The elder wizards lost their powers. The realm was dying. The High Council called for Kael. "Our magic is gone," the Headmaster coughed weakly. "You know the ancient maps. You must bring back the Sunstone to restore our light." Kael's knees shook. He was terrified. He was just a magicless boy sent into mortal danger. Still, his people were counting on him. He packed his bag.

---

## Part 3 — The Whispering Woods

Kael left the safe walls of Ultimyr. The outside world was loud and scary. He entered the Whispering Woods, jumping at every broken branch. "I cannot do this," he whispered, hugging his heavy book. Suddenly, a giant, angry shadow wolf jumped onto the path. It growled, showing long, sharp teeth. Kael fell backward, covering his face. He prepared for the worst. He had no sword, no spells, and absolutely no courage to fight back.

---

## Part 4 — Exort

Just as the wolf leaped, a glowing red orb shot out of the bushes. "Burn, you ugly dog!" a squeaky voice yelled. The red wisp slammed into the wolf's nose, exploding in hot sparks. The wolf yelped and ran away. Kael opened his eyes. The wisp hovered, burning with fierce energy. "I am Exort!" the wisp declared proudly. "Did you see that? I roasted him! Why were you just sitting there, book-boy?"

---

## Part 5 — The Broken Bridge

Exort sat on Kael's shoulder, buzzing with heat. Kael explained his dangerous quest to find the Sunstone. Exort sparked brightly. "I love danger! I will protect you." They walked until they reached a rushing river. The bridge was completely destroyed. The dark water moved way too fast to swim. Kael sighed in defeat. "We are stuck," Kael said. Exort groaned. "Don't quit now! I can try to evaporate the whole river. Just give me a week."

---

## Part 6 — Wex

Before Exort could try, a streak of purple light zipped across the water. "Too slow, hothead!" a fast, mocking voice echoed. It was Wex, an arcane wisp sparking with electricity. "I could cross this a hundred times before you boil a single drop, Exort." Wex flew back and forth, leaving a glowing purple trail that hardened into a magical bridge. "Hurry up, slowpokes!" Wex chirped, bouncing excitedly in the air.

---

## Part 7 — The Scorched Desert

Kael was amazed. Wex and Exort were brothers, separated by a wild magic storm. With Wex joining them, the journey moved much faster. But as they entered the Scorched Desert, the sun was brutal. "Keep up, Exort, your flames are dragging!" Wex teased. "Shut up, sparky, or I will melt you!" Exort yelled back. Kael's boots sank heavily into the sand. He was exhausted and dropped to his knees. "It is too far," he panted.

---

## Part 8 — Quas

A heavy, cold shadow fell over Kael. A glowing blue wisp drifted down from the sky, chilling the hot desert air. "Silence," a deep, slow voice rumbled. It was Quas. He dropped a freezing mist that cooled Kael's face. "You two give me a headache," Quas told his brothers. Exort huffed, and Wex zipped in a quick circle. "Took you long enough, ice-cube," Wex muttered. The three triplet brothers were finally together again.

---

## Part 9 — The Anchor

With the triplets together, something amazing happened. Kael felt a strange energy connecting him to the wisps. They were powerful but chaotic. Kael realized he was meant to be their anchor. He gently raised his hands. The brothers spun around his fingers. Exort's fire, Wex's speed, and Quas's cold flowed into Kael's mind. He was not creating the magic, but his mind could guide it. For the first time, Kael felt truly powerful.

---

## Part 10 — The Goblins

They finally reached the deep canyon holding the Sunstone. However, a gang of nasty goblins blocked the path. The goblins raised rusty swords and charged. Kael's old fear returned, but he looked at his three glowing friends. "Let's fry them!" Exort shouted. "No, let's dizzy them!" Wex spun. "Hold steady," Quas rumbled. Kael took a deep breath. "Help me," he said softly. He pulled Quas and Exort together into his hands.

---

## Part 11 — Ice Wall

Kael pointed at the goblins. "Ice Wall!" he shouted. Quas poured out heavy frost, while Exort added a thin layer of heat. "Why do I have to be the puddle?" Exort complained, as his heat melted the ice just enough to stick the goblins' feet to the ground. "Just do your job, hothead," Quas rumbled slowly. A massive block of ice erupted, freezing the goblins in place. Kael stared at his hands. He had actually cast a spell.

---

## Part 12 — The Stone Golem

Kael bravely walked into the dark cave. At the center, the golden Sunstone glowed. But sleeping beside it was a giant Stone Golem. As Kael reached out, the golem woke up. It roared, shaking the cave. Kael trembled. This was a monster from his nightmares. "Don't freeze up now, book-boy!" Wex zipped around his head. "We can take this overgrown rock!" Exort yelled. Kael nodded. He could not be a coward now.

---

## Part 13 — Chaos Meteor

The golem raised a massive stone fist. Kael quickly called on Exort twice and Wex once. "Chaos Meteor!" Kael commanded. Exort formed a giant ball of raging fire. "Push me, purple boy!" Exort yelled. "With pleasure!" Wex laughed, using his speed to launch the fireball down from the cave ceiling. The flaming rock rolled directly over the golem, crushing its thick armor. The golem staggered backwards, heavily damaged but still furiously charging at Kael.

---

## Part 14 — Deafening Blast

Kael needed to finish this right now. He called upon all three brothers at once. "Let's show him our best trick!" Wex cheered. "Maximum heat!" Exort roared. "Absolute cold," Quas hummed. The three wisps spun into a perfect, blinding sphere of magic in Kael's palms. "Deafening Blast!" Kael thrust his hands forward. A massive wave of sonic energy, fire, and cold slammed into the golem. The monster shattered into a thousand harmless pebbles.

---

## Part 15 — The Sunstone

The cave went perfectly silent. Kael safely picked up the warm Sunstone. He had done it. When Kael returned to Ultimyr, the Sunstone cleared the dark fog instantly. Pure magic returned to the realm. The elder wizards bowed to the young boy in deep respect. Kael was no longer the useless child. He was a brave hero. Exort, Wex, and Quas teased each other happily around his head. Together, they were a perfect team.

## Story structure notes

Kept here so future edits do not accidentally break the chain.

- **Audience:** the young end of the folder. Closest in tone to `lina-golden-rolling-pin.md` and
  `juggernaut-sixty-one-carvings.md`. No swearing, no real horror, nobody dies.
- **One goal, stated early:** Part 2 gives the quest out loud — bring back the Sunstone and the
  fog lifts. It is the same goal in Part 15. Nothing is held back from the reader at any point.
- **Kael's arc in one sentence:** the boy with no magic of his own turns out to be the only one
  who can hold someone else's. He never gains power. He gains control.
- **This is an origin story, and the reveal is for the player, not for Kael.** Kael is never
  called Invoker inside the story. The reader works it out from the three orb names and the three
  real spells, which is why those six words must stay exactly as the game writes them. The title
  of the file is the only place the connection is stated.
- **Shape:** Parts 1–3 are the weak boy alone. Parts 4, 6 and 8 each introduce one wisp and are
  built the same way on purpose — a sudden rescue, then an insult. Part 9 is the turn. Parts
  10–14 are three fights of rising size. Part 15 is the homecoming.
- **The three-part introduction pattern must stay.** Each wisp arrives at the exact moment Kael
  gives up: the wolf (Part 3→4), the broken bridge (Part 5→6), the desert (Part 7→8). Three
  times, same beat, bigger each time. Part 9 only works because the reader has seen the pattern
  three times and expects a fourth rescue — and instead Kael does it himself.
- **Setups and payoffs — do not remove these:**
  - **"Not a single spark left his fingers"** (Part 1) → the brothers spin around his fingers and
    he guides them (Part 9) → he casts three spells with his hands (Parts 11, 13, 14). The story
    keeps its promise: no spark ever does leave his fingers. The magic is always theirs.
  - **"book-boy"** (Part 4) → the same word is used to push him into the fight he is most scared
    of (Part 12). It starts as an insult and ends as encouragement, with no change of wording.
  - **"He spent his days hiding behind giant piles of books"** (Part 1) → "You know the ancient
    maps" is the exact reason the Council picks him (Part 2) → he hugs the heavy book for comfort
    in the woods (Part 3). The reading is not a joke about him being useless; it is why he is sent.
  - **The three spells are built from the right orbs, and a player will check.** Ice Wall uses
    Quas and Exort (Parts 10–11). Chaos Meteor uses Exort twice and Wex once, and Part 13 says so
    out loud. Deafening Blast uses all three at once (Part 14). **Do not change which orbs go
    into which spell** — that accuracy is most of the reason the last third lands.

    Part 10 deliberately says only "he pulled Quas and Exort together" rather than giving exact
    numbers. In the game Ice Wall is two Quas and one Exort, and an earlier edit spelled that out;
    it was reverted on purpose, because the looser line reads better and the two orbs are still
    the right ones. Leave it loose. Chaos Meteor keeps its exact count because the sentence needs
    the detail anyway.
  - **Each wisp's one trait is set up and then used to win.** Exort's heat burns the wolf (Part 4)
    and melts the ice in Ice Wall (Part 11). Wex's speed builds the bridge (Part 6) and launches
    the meteor (Part 13). Quas's cold saves Kael in the desert (Part 8) and freezes the goblins
    (Part 11). Nobody gets a new power in the last act.
  - **The brothers were separated by a magic storm** (Part 7) → they are back together at the end
    of Part 8, which is why Part 9 is possible. Kael's quest quietly fixes their problem before it
    fixes his.
  - **The bickering never stops, including after the victory** (Part 15). That is the point of the
    last line. Do not give them a solemn ending.
- **Canon notes for future edits:**
  - Quas is ice, Wex is lightning and speed, Exort is fire. Never swap them.
  - Quas, Wex and Exort being three talking brothers is this story's invention, not Dota canon,
    where they are Invoker's orbs. It is a children's telling and it is allowed to stay, but do
    not add lore that contradicts the game more deeply than this already does.
  - Ultimyr is canon. Whispering Woods, Scorched Desert and the Sunstone are invented for this
    story.
- **Never explain the ending.** Part 15 states what happened and stops. Do not add a line telling
  the reader that Kael became Invoker.
