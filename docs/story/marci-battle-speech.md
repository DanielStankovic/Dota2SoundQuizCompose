## Localisation notes

**Hero names are NEVER translated.** Keep them exactly as written, in every language:

- Marci

If a locale has an official transliteration of "Marci", use it. Do not replace her with
a local name, a nickname, or Mirana.

Other do-not-translate names:

- None. This Issue has no place names, no shop signs, no invented people.

Canon Dota locations:

- None in this file. Do not add the Nightsilver Woods, the Moon Court, Highground, or
  any other Dota place in order to "explain" the speech.

Items that *should* be translated, with care:

- **"Marci's Battle Speech"** — the story title, and the whole joke. Keep **Marci**.
  Translate **Battle Speech** with everyday words: a talk you give to your side
  standing up, just before a fight. Not lecture, not poem, not song, not whistle, not
  "tactical briefing". The title must still sound like a real story sitting next to
  *Lina and the Golden Rolling Pin*. If a literal "battle speech" is clumsy, write a
  new title that does the same job: it names Marci, and it names a speech before a
  fight. A joke that lands beats a stiff literal.
- **"Marci has a few words before the battle."** — the picker synopsis. Translate the
  meaning with everyday words. It must still sound like she is about to talk, and
  like a fight is about to start. Do not mention whistling, muteness, or jokes in the
  synopsis. The reader is not supposed to be in on it yet.
- **"Before the Battle"** — the part title. Translate the meaning. Keep it short.
  Same rule as the synopsis: it is a speech heading, not a hint.

### The page itself — this is not English prose

Marci has **no spoken voicelines** in the game. Do not insert a catchphrase from another
hero, and do not write a real speech in the target language. The Chapter body is only
whistle sounds, written as short sentences with periods, in the same paragraphs as the
English file.

`Tweet` / `Fweet` / `Fweeeet` in the English file are how a whistle looks on the page,
not the English verb *tweet* (as in social media) and not a bird's name. **English left
inside a Chinese (or any other) page reads like a bug**, so do not copy the letters
`Tweet` into a locale that does not use that word. Write the local onomatopoeia for a
human whistle or a small bright peep — the sound a children's book would use for
someone whistling, not for a phone notification.

Keep the **rhythm**, not the letters:

- A short sound, a pair of sounds, then one longer sound, in the same places as the
  English.
- **`Twe twe tweet.`** is her laugh: short, short, long. Two of them, same rhythm both
  times.
- **`Tweet?`** is the only question. Keep the question mark. It opens the third
  paragraph on purpose.
- **`Fweeeet.`** is a longer whistle than `Tweet`. Keep a longer written sound in those
  spots. Do not flatten every whistle into one word.
- The last line is thanks: two short sounds and a full stop, alone on the line. Do not
  add a word after it.

Do not turn the page into sentences with meaning. Do not add a narrator. Do not add
"hello" or "thank you". Keep the **paragraphs**. Do not break the Chapter into one
whistle per line.

**Swearing — there is none in this story, on purpose.** Do not add any, in any
language.

### Style guidance — simple English is a hard rule

Most readers are **not native English speakers**, and the text will be machine-translated
into many languages. The English around this Chapter (title, synopsis, part title) is
deliberately plain. Keep it that way when editing, and match it when translating.

- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence.
- **Everyday words only.** The title says *battle speech*, not oration, address,
  keynote, or tactical briefing.
- **No literary or poetic language.**
- **Do not raise the register when translating.** If the title sounds like a school
  textbook, it is wrong.
- **There is no dialogue and no narration in the Chapter body.** Keep it that way.

## Story structure notes

Kept here so future edits do not accidentally break the chain.

- **One goal, start to finish:** Marci gives a speech before a fight. The title
  states it. The last line ends it. Told in order, no flashbacks, no mystery to hold
  in your head.
- **Marci's arc in one sentence:** the mute hero delivers a full battle speech
  without a single word.
- **Cliffhangers:** none. This is one part. The part still **ends on its strongest
  line** — `Tweet tweet.` alone, as thanks.
- **Setups and payoffs — do not remove these:**
  - **The title and the synopsis are the setup.** They promise a speech before a
    battle. If either one mentions whistling, muteness, or a joke, the payoff is gone
    before the reader opens the Chapter.
  - **The Chapter body is the payoff.** It is only whistles. Do not add English (or
    any other language's) words to "help".
  - **`Tweet?` opens paragraph three.** That is the turn, the way a real part turns.
    Keep it the only question.
  - **`Twe twe tweet.` is the laugh**, twice — once in the long second paragraph, once
    near the end. Same rhythm both times.
  - **The last line is thanks**, two short sounds, its own paragraph. Do not move it
    up into the fourth paragraph.
- **Length:** about a hundred whistle-words, in four paragraphs plus a last line.
  That is the same size as a part in `lina-golden-rolling-pin.md`. If it is ever
  shortened, it stops looking like a Chapter and starts looking like a caption.
- **Canon:** Marci is mute and communicates by whistling. Effort grunts from other
  games are the wrong voice. Stay on whistles.
- **Never explain the ending.**
