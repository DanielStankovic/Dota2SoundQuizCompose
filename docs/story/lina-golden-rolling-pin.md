# Lina and the Golden Rolling Pin

A fifteen-part story about Lina, told in simple English for a young audience.
Each part is roughly 100 words and is meant to be delivered one part per Hero Journey level
(parts 1–15 → levels 1–15).

---

## Localisation notes

**Hero names are NEVER translated.** Keep them exactly as written, in every language:

- Lina
- Rylai
- Davion
- Kunkka
- Lion
- Ogre Magi

This also protects the "Maggs" nickname joke in Part 2 and Part 7. The nickname works
because children misread the written word *Magi* as *Maggs*. Since "Ogre Magi" stays in
English in every locale, the misreading still makes sense and must **not** be adapted or
localised. Keep "Maggs" as-is too.

Other do-not-translate terms:

- **Icewrack Valley** — place name, leave as-is or transliterate the way the game
  transliterates "Icewrack" in that locale. Do not translate the meaning.
- **LINA'S** — shop sign in Part 15.
- **OGRE MAGI — BREAD** — shop sign in Part 2.
- **LION'S FINE CAKES** — shop sign in Part 6.

Items that *should* be translated normally:

- **The Golden Rolling Pin** — the contest name. Translate the meaning; it is not a hero name.
- **BAKER WANTED. MUST LIKE HEAT.** — job notice in Part 1.
- **GOLDEN ROLLING PIN — POSTPONED UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE.** — council notice in Part 12.
- All part titles.

Style guidance for translators: keep sentences short and words everyday. The original avoids
poetic or literary vocabulary on purpose, because most readers are not native English speakers.
Do not raise the register. There is almost no dialogue; keep it that way.

## Part 1 — Nobody Will Hire Her

Lina had eleven coins, one bag, and a very bad reputation.

It was not her fault. She was made of fire. When she worked in a library, the books went up like dry leaves. When she worked on a farm, the hay did too. The last town paid her to leave, and they paid her quickly.

So she walked down into Icewrack Valley — a tiny town squeezed between a lake and a mountain, where the houses stand shoulder to shoulder and everybody knows everybody — and she read the job board. One notice was left. It was stained with butter.

**BAKER WANTED. MUST LIKE HEAT.**

---

## Part 2 — Maggs

The bakery belonged to an ogre. A real one, two heads tall, with two heads.

He was a magi — an ogre wizard — so when he opened the shop forty years ago he painted a proud sign: **OGRE MAGI — BREAD**. The children of Icewrack Valley could not read it properly. They read *Magi* out loud as "Maggs," and that was that. Nobody in town had called him anything else since.

Maggs had one dream: to retire. But his oven had cracked in spring and he could not afford a new one.

Then a red-haired woman walked in and lit her own hand like a lamp.

---

## Part 3 — Forty Loaves of Charcoal

Her first day did not go well.

Lina put her hands under the baking tray and thought, *a little heat*. The tray turned white. Then orange. Then it stopped being a tray.

By noon she had made forty loaves of bread. All forty were black. They were so black that a crow landed on one, apologised, and left.

Maggs did not shout. He lined the loaves up on the counter like little soldiers and looked at them sadly. Then he handed her a fresh bowl and told her to try again.

Lina had never in her life been given a second try.

---

## Part 4 — Low and Slow

Bread, it turned out, was harder than dragons.

Dragons you hit with everything you have. Bread wants the opposite of everything you have. Bread wants patience.

So Lina learned to be small. She held a flame the size of a candle for one whole hour without letting it grow. Her hands shook. She hated it. She did it anyway.

In her bag were six letters from her sister Rylai, who was made of ice, and who she had not spoken to in four years. All six were unopened. She kept meaning to read them.

On the ninth day, a loaf came out gold.

---

## Part 5 — The Man with the Flour

The flour came every Tuesday, by dragon, which is a silly way to deliver flour.

The rider was a knight named Davion. He had good armour, bad hair, and the confidence of a man who has never once been nervous — until he walked into the bakery, saw Lina, and walked straight into the door frame.

He said the dragon did it. The dragon looked away.

After that, the flour started arriving on Tuesdays *and* Thursdays. Maggs did not need flour twice a week. Maggs said nothing, because his right head was a romantic and his left head liked the extra flour.

---

## Part 6 — The Lion Next Door

In summer, a shop opened across the narrow street.

**LION'S FINE CAKES.** It was run by a thin, grinning man with glowing eyes who had never baked anything in his life. His cakes were made of pure magic — no flour, no oven, no firewood, nothing. They tasted like whatever you wanted most, for about ten seconds. Then they tasted like nothing, and you were hungry again, so you bought another.

Icewrack Valley loved them. Icewrack Valley queued around the corner for them.

Lina's bread was better and everybody knew it. But her bread did not sparkle, so her shop sat empty all summer.

---

## Part 7 — One Shop Stays

By autumn the town council had a problem: two bakeries, seven hundred people, one tiny street.

So they announced a contest. The Golden Rolling Pin. One day, one bake, three judges. The winner would be the official baker of Icewrack Valley. The loser had to close.

The clerk read the paperwork out loud, and the owner of the second bakery was listed by his full legal name: *Ogre Magi*. Nobody clapped, because nobody knew who that was. Then somebody whispered *that's Maggs*, and the whole hall clapped at once.

Maggs went pale, both heads. Lina signed the paper hard enough to scorch it.

---

## Part 8 — Three Judges and a Captain

The judges were picked by lottery, which is how you end up with a panel like this.

First: a very old woman who had eaten bread in Icewrack Valley for eighty-one years and disliked most of it.

Second: a travelling wizard who stole everyone's recipes just by watching, then claimed he invented them.

Third: a sailor called Kunkka, who had no qualifications at all, but who once ate a rum cake so good he cried about it in three different ports.

Lina studied all three for a week. Then she decided her entry would have rum in it.

---

## Part 9 — Thursdays

They practised at night, after the shop closed.

Davion was useless. He could not measure anything. He put salt in the sugar jar twice and lied about it both times. But he was good at the one thing Lina was terrible at, which was stopping.

Every night around midnight he took the tray out of her hands, made her sit down, and made her eat something. Then they ate the mistakes together on the step, looking at the lake.

On the last Thursday before the contest, she realised she was not practising for the contest anymore. She kept practising anyway.

---

## Part 10 — Cheating

Two nights before the contest, somebody broke into the bakery.

They did not take money. They took her recipe card and her good copper tray, and they left the door wide open, which let the rain in, which ruined the flour.

Lina stood in the wet kitchen at four in the morning with a glowing fist. Across the street, one window was still lit, and the light in it was faintly purple.

She got as far as the doorway. Then Maggs put one enormous hand on her shoulder and told her the oven was the only weapon they needed.

She unclenched her fist. Slowly.

---

## Part 11 — Rylai

The next morning, her sister arrived.

Rylai had written six letters and got nothing back, so in the end she simply walked to Icewrack Valley herself to say sorry in person. She brought a gift, carefully wrapped: a piece of true glacier ice from home, the cold heart of the mountain where they were born.

She stood on the doorstep and could not get a single word out. Her hands shook. Ice magic follows the feelings of the one who carries it — and Rylai was terrified.

The ice in her hands cracked.

What came out of it was not a snowflake. It was winter.

---

## Part 12 — Postponed Until Further Notice

The storm swallowed Icewrack Valley before lunch.

Snow buried the street, the lake froze grey, and the cold put out every fire in every house in town. By evening the council nailed a notice to the hall door: **GOLDEN ROLLING PIN — POSTPONED UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE.** No new date. Both bakeries still had to close, one day, eventually. Nobody cared about any of it.

So Lina opened the bakery doors wide, in a blizzard, which everyone told her was insane.

She lit the cracked old oven. And she kept it lit for three days and three nights without stopping once.

---

## Part 13 — Firewood

Magic cakes cannot feed a frozen town. Lion learned this fast. His customers spat them out and went looking for real bread, and his shop had no oven, no chimney and no wood, because he had never needed any.

By the second night his shop was an icebox and the bakery was the only warm room in Icewrack Valley. Lion was far too proud to ask. So he turned up with an armful of firewood instead, called it a *trade*, and set it down.

Then he went back for more. Four trips. He complained the entire time.

On the last trip, he quietly returned the copper tray.

---

## Part 14 — The Longest Bake

By then Lina was fading. Fire burns fuel, and she was the fuel. Her hands had gone grey and the flame was dropping.

Rylai sat down beside her and finally did the one thing she came to do. The storm was hers, so she took it back — hour after hour, pulling the cold out of the air and into herself, until the snow stopped and the sky cracked open blue.

The whole town walked out into the sunshine alive. Every single person.

The contest was never rescheduled. Instead the old woman who had eaten bread for eighty-one years put the Golden Rolling Pin on the counter, said it was the best bread of her life, and left before anyone could argue.

---

## Part 15 — Tuesdays Forever

Lion sold his shop and left to sell something else to someone else. He wrote once, from the city. Lina wrote back, which surprised them both.

Maggs finally retired. He cried about it for a week — his right head started it. The sign above the door says **LINA'S** now, but the old one hangs inside, because the town still calls the shop Maggs's out of habit, and probably always will.

Rylai comes every winter and stays through the cold. They read each other's letters out loud now, including the old six.

Davion still brings the flour on Tuesdays. Last week he asked her to dinner.

She said yes.

## Story structure notes

Kept here so future edits do not accidentally break the chain.

- **One goal, start to finish:** save the bakery. Told in order, no flashbacks, no mystery to
  hold in your head.
- **Lina's arc in one sentence:** the girl who burns everything learns to be gentle — and that
  is exactly what saves the town. The heroic act in Part 12 is holding a *small* flame steady
  for three days, not blasting anything.
- **Cliffhangers that pull the reader to the next level:** the empty shop (6), the closure
  threat (7), the break-in (10), the sneeze that becomes winter (11), Lina fading (14).
- **Setups and payoffs — do not remove these:**
  - Six unopened letters (Part 4) → Rylai's arrival (Part 11) → letters read aloud (Part 15).
  - Lion's cakes need no oven and no firewood (Part 6) → that backfires in the blizzard and is
    why he hauls wood (Part 13).
  - The purple light across the street (Part 10) → Lion was the thief; he returns the copper
    tray without comment (Part 13).
  - Rylai causes the storm (Part 11) and is the one who ends it (Part 14).
  - The "Maggs" nickname is explained in Part 2 and paid off in Part 7.
